# Zoonk's design system on Apple platforms

Main's design system (`apps/playground/src/design-system/`, values in `packages/ui/src/styles/globals.css`) says what each thing means and how the content looks. On Apple platforms the content layer takes the same values and anatomy through the app's own roles and pieces, and the chrome stays the system's, so iOS still handles Dynamic Type, Bold Text, Dark Mode, Increase Contrast, Reduce Motion, Liquid Glass and new devices. Screens never spell a value: no hex colors, no fixed font sizes, no radius numbers, no custom controls where a native one exists.

The app's code for this lives in `apps/apple/Zoonk/DesignSystem/`: the generated colors and radii, the type roles, and main's shared pieces drawn with native building blocks. A Debug build launched with `ZOONK_DEBUG_GALLERY=1` (or a section's name: `colors`, `text`, `buttons`, `chips`, `lists`, `panels`, `meters`, `marks`, `options`, `fields`) shows every role and piece in one scroll, the native side of main's `/design` and Android's gallery. When you change a piece or add one, add it there and look at it in light, dark, Increase Contrast, an accessibility size, Bold Text and right to left.

## Ground rules

- **Minimum iOS 26.** Liquid Glass, `.glass` button styles, `ConcentricRectangle` and `safeAreaBar` need no `#available` checks.
- **One source.** `pnpm palette` regenerates both apps from `globals.css`: here every color role (`Resources/Assets.xcassets/Palette`, Display P3, with light, dark and both Increase Contrast appearances), the `AccentColor` and `Radius.swift`, and Android's theme with them (the generators share `scripts/theme/`). Never edit those outputs by hand: change `globals.css` and run it ([README](../../../../apps/apple/README.md#design-tokens)).
- **The tint is ink.** `AccentColor` is main's `primary`, and `ASSETCATALOG_COMPILER_GLOBAL_ACCENT_COLOR_NAME` applies it. Links, the selected tab, spinners and the system's own controls are ink, as on main. Don't set `.tint` on screens, except for toggles, iOS's one exception to main's look (see Components).
- **Color means something or stays neutral.** Before you color something, find its role below. If none fits, use `Color(.foreground)`, `.mutedForeground` or `.faint`.
- **Never color alone.** A word, an SF Symbol or a shape says the same thing.

- `.weight(_:)`, `.bold()`, `.monospaced()` (Geist Mono), `.monospacedDigit()` (Geist with tabular figures) and `.italic()` work on a role as `Font`'s do. A number inside a sentence keeps the sentence's role with `.monospacedDigit()`. Changing numbers take `.contentTransition(.numericText())`.
- Change the weight of text that inherits its role with `.typeWeight(_:)`, not `.fontWeight(_:)` or `.bold()`, so Bold Text still adds to it. A piece of an interpolated `Text` takes `Text.typeWeight(_:legibility:)` with its view's `legibilityWeight`, and Markdown takes `Text(_:legibility:)`, which keeps strong emphasis heavier than the text around it. `.fontWeight` stays for SF Symbols.
- Text that can't take a view modifier (a `Canvas`'s `context.resolve`) uses `.font(role.font(in: context.environment))`; a font inside an `AttributedString` uses `role.attributeFont`.
- A role names one job and carries its size, weight, tracking and, where main sets one (a lesson's text, an essay's box), its line height. A place that needs a size or line height no role has gets a named role in `Typography.swift`, never a number or `.lineSpacing` at the call site.
- Never write `.font(_:)` for app text. System-drawn UI keeps the system font: navigation bar titles (large titles too), tab bar labels, alerts, menus, pickers, the keyboard. A page shows its title in Geist through `PageHeader`. An SF Symbol takes the role of the text beside it, plus `.imageScale` if it needs to change size.
- Scripts Geist lacks (Japanese, Korean, Greek) fall back to the system font, as on the web.

## Shape and spacing

Main's radii (`--radius`) are generated into `DesignSystem/Radius.swift` with the palette: `Radius.sm`, `.md`, `.lg`, `.option`, `.xl` and `.xxl`. Draw corners as `.continuous` rounded rectangles of those constants, never a number: buttons and fields `lg` (small buttons and chips `md`), answer options and stat tiles `option`, panels `xl`. Nothing is a capsule except avatars, dots and rings. Inside a container with a shape (a sheet, a glass group), use `ConcentricRectangle()` so corners nest.

`ZoonkList` gives a page its margins and the space between sections; inside a row or panel, take the spacing main's piece uses (`packages/learn/src/_components`). Lines are 1pt in `.border` or `.input`; a list's separators are the system's hairline. No shadows in content; sheets and menus keep the system's.

- `StatusMark` has one scaled width, and `ListRow` gives its leading marks one width, so a list's words start in one column.
- A width that's a share of the column (main's `max-w-[85%]` bubble, a `width: 60%` bar) is a `ShareRow`. `containerRelativeFrame` measures the scroll view, which is wider than a capped lesson column on iPad and at accessibility sizes.

## Accessibility text sizes

From the first accessibility size text grows past what a row can hold side by side, so layouts change there, as Apple's apps do. One rule decides it, `@Environment(\.stacksContent)`, and `DesignSystem/AccessibilityLayout.swift` holds the shared pieces that follow it. Never switch a layout on `isAccessibilitySize` yourself:

- Things side by side stack, text above what sat beside it: `AccessibilityStack` (`stackedSpacing` when the stacked gap differs). A title with a value at its end puts `.frame(maxWidth: .infinity, alignment: .leading)` on the title; a section header like that is `SectionTitle`.
- A mark, its text and something at the row's end (a chevron, a drag handle, a badge) are a `MarkedRow`: stacked, the mark and the end share the first line and the text takes the width under them. A mark without text, such as a `GoalRing`, passes `alignment: .center`.
- Where content changes beyond the stack itself (a value's own alignment, a fixed width side by side), read `stacksContent`. Off a hinge, two panes or grid columns become one column on it too (`PaneSpace.pages(primaryShare:stacked:)`). At iPhone Duo's hinge each page is about a phone wide, so a page and what supports it keep a page each and their text stacks inside them; only a spread becomes one column there (`PaneSpace.panes(primaryShare:stacked:)`).
- Stacked, a list row would flow a label's icon into its title's first line and push aside the first line of each text or control in the title. `StackingLabelStyle` keeps the icon beside a title that fits its line and puts it above one that wraps, as in a `MarkedRow`. In a list, a `LabeledContent` lays out a label in its own label itself, with the system's flow, so a list row with an icon and a value is an `IconLabeledContent`: a label around the `LabeledContent`, which looks the same side by side, keeps the system's title and subtitle styling, and takes the label style stacked. Outside a list, a `LabeledContent`'s label takes the style as it is. Stacked, the label style lays out a title as written, so a label's title with a note under it styles the note itself (a `VStack` with spacing 4, the note in `.note` and `.mutedForeground`); a `LabeledContent` styles its own.
- The accessibility audit grows the text size and loses text that's replaced on the way: text inside an `if` on `stacksContent`, or in a `ViewThatFits` that holds two copies of it. It reports that text as "Dynamic Type font sizes are partially unsupported", with no element. Where a screen the audit covers changes layout as text grows, keep the text one view and switch the layout around it, with `AnyLayout` (`AccessibilityStack`) or a custom `Layout` (`StackingLabelStyle`).
- A sentence built from pieces in a `FlowLayout` (words, blanks) uses `alignment: .firstTextBaseline`, so a piece that wraps keeps its neighbors on its first line.
- Text buttons side by side go in a `ButtonRow`: stacked, each is a regular-size rounded rectangle as wide as the row, like an alert's buttons.
- Buttons are rounded rectangles, not capsules: a wrapped capsule's round ends take the width its words need. Zoonk's button styles always are; a system-styled button (glass) takes the rounded rectangle from `zoonkStyles()` at accessibility sizes.
- Neither the button shape, the label style, the `GroupBox` style nor the default type role reaches into a presentation, so the app's root and the root of every presented screen (each `.sheet`, `.fullScreenCover` and `.popover`, the router's included) apply them with `zoonkStyles()`.
- A bar over the content (a `safeAreaBar`) grows with Dynamic Type like the content: never cap its text with `dynamicTypeSize(_:)`. Apple keeps fixed sizes for system bars, and the accessibility audit flags capped text anywhere else. Keep it short with its layout instead: chips stay one row that scrolls sideways, and a field and its button stack like any side-by-side content. Only controls that can't stack, such as a call's controls, show their symbols alone and their titles in the Large Content Viewer (`accessibilityShowsLargeContentViewer()`).
- A plot's labels, placed by value in a frame that doesn't grow, stop growing at the largest standard size with `chartsPlotText()`, where they still fit beside each other. The words and readouts around the plot keep growing.

## Liquid Glass

- Glass belongs to the navigation and control layer, never to content: `TabView`, `NavigationStack` bars, toolbars, sheets, menus and floating controls. They adopt it on their own.
- Don't paint backgrounds on bars, tab bars or toolbars. Custom backgrounds there break glass and the scroll edge effect.
- Content (rows, panels, readouts, answer options) stays opaque on the page and the design system's surfaces.
- `.glassProminent` is not the main action. With the ink tint its white label vanishes on dark mode's white glass, and recoloring it doesn't help because glass draws labels vibrantly (they turn gray). Use `.mainAction`, in a `safeAreaBar` when it floats.
- Several custom glass controls together go in one `GlassEffectContainer`.
- Group toolbar items by job, separate groups with `ToolbarSpacer(.fixed)`, and give every icon-only item an accessibility label.
- Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast restyle glass on their own. Don't fight them with custom materials.

## Motion

Everyday screens stay still. Feedback uses the system's: control states, `.contentTransition`, `.symbolEffect`. Celebrations move once, only when something is earned:

| Main                                  | SwiftUI                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| quick/base, ease-out (a state change) | Native control animations, or `.animation(.snappy, value:)`                                           |
| spring (something earned lands)       | `.bouncy`: it is SwiftUI's `Spring(duration: 0.5, bounce: 0.3)`, which main's `--ease-spring` samples |
| fill and count-up (700ms)             | `withAnimation(.easeOut(duration: 0.7))` with `.contentTransition(.numericText())` on the number      |
| pop (a mark swells once)              | `.symbolEffect(.bounce, value:)`                                                                      |

Reduce Motion: system controls and symbol effects adapt on their own. A celebration you animate yourself reads `@Environment(\.accessibilityReduceMotion)` and shows its end state with no animation (`withAnimation(reduceMotion ? nil : .bouncy)`).

## Every device

- Choose layouts by size class and available space, never by device. `userInterfaceIdiom`, screen bounds, orientation and known screen sizes are wrong on iPhone Duo and in resizable iPad windows. Measure with `onGeometryChange`, and use `ViewThatFits` and native containers.
- **iPhone Duo** (iOS 27.1 SDK, which Xcode 27.1 builds with): on the inner display the app fills the screen, the status bar sits on the right edge, and the tab bar stands vertically below it, showing **icons only**. Every tab's SF Symbol must read on its own, with its title kept as the label for VoiceOver. Content keeps clear of the bar through the safe area. Don't crop media with `.aspectRatio(contentMode: .fill)` without a focal point.
- **iPad**: `sidebarAdaptable` tabs become a top tab bar or a sidebar, whose Study group lists main's study places in main's order. A page's `ZoonkList` is one centered column with the phone's structure, as main's tablet portrait; rows and panels keep the same roles, and grids gain columns as the width grows. Without a hinge, `PaneSplit` and `PaneBeside` put a page and what supports it side by side only from `PaneSpace.minimumPagesWidth`, just past the widest iPad in portrait, so any iPad in portrait or a window that size reads as one column (Today then carries its main action in its header, as main's tablet portrait does); a spread keeps its own rule.
- **Simulators**: a headless iPhone Duo renders black until something attaches a display (agent-device or Simulator). `agent-device fold closed|half-open|open` sets the pose (half-open is the partial fold); when it reports that the hinge read-back failed, the pose usually still applied, so check a screenshot (a sheet moves to one side of the fold while it's partially folded) or the simulator's log, which records the angle the fold settled at (`xcrun simctl spawn <udid> log show --last 5m | grep ContinuousFold` shows a line such as `settleOpen=YES (lastAngle=130.0)`). `agent-device orientation` reports success but rotates neither display, so the inner display in portrait and tabletop need Device Hub. A headless run can still rotate it: a script can click Device Hub's Controls menu (Rotate Left or Rotate Right) through System Events with `osascript`. On an iPad, agent-device can drag a full-screen window's corner to shrink it but never drag it back out, and iPadOS keeps that size across relaunches and reinstalls, so don't shrink a shared simulator's window that way. `simctl io <udid> screenshot --display=primary-1` captures the inner display and `--display=primary` the outer one. `simctl ui <udid> appearance|increase_contrast|content_size` sets each mode for checks.

### iPhone Duo

Open, the Duo reads like an open wallet: two panes either side of the fold, each about an iPhone wide, so the phone's views go in unchanged. `PaneSplit` places them; its `single` builder is the phone's one column. Folding or unfolding keeps the learner's place.

[Book foldables](../../zoonk-design/references/foldables.md) says what goes in each pane for each kind of screen, the same as on Android's book foldables. `PaneSplit`'s `primaryShare`, the leading pane's share where there's no hinge, is 0.55 for main and supporting screens and 0.5 for every other kind.

- In the lesson player, the stage's leading page shows the step's `.screen` part and the trailing page its `.turn` part, with the action bar at the trailing page's bottom; a step decides what goes in each part from `isPaneSplit`, keeping the phone's order in one column. The stage is a spread: where its text stacks it becomes that one column, which keeps to the page before the hinge with the action bar at its bottom. A canvas on the leading page is made anew on every fold, so canvases keep the learner's work in `@ActivityState` (the player's `ActivityWork`, kept for the screen's answer round), and views the learner types into stay in the part that lives through a fold, the turn, in one place within it.
- A side panel (the lesson's Ask, the buddy's day) is the trailing pane where the screen splits (`PaneSplit`'s, or the player's stage), never an `.inspector`: an inspector presented while the Duo is partially folded leaves the main column running under it, and SwiftUI turns a presented inspector into a sheet when the width turns compact, covering the screen. Where the screen is one column the panel is a sheet, shown only when it was opened there (the player's `isAskingInSheet`), so closing the Duo never covers the screen with one.
- Where a pane's views hold what the learner types or opens (a form with an editor, rows that expand, a list's next page loading), use `PaneBeside` instead of `PaneSplit`: its content keeps its identity as the other pane comes and goes, with that pane on the leading side (the account sheet's hub) or the trailing one (Today's examples, the notebook's fixed list).
- A pane is about a phone wide even where the size class is regular. A view inside it that picks a wide layout from the regular size class overflows, so give the pane `.environment(\.horizontalSizeClass, .compact)` (as Statistics does) or choose by the measured width.
- At the hinge, `PaneSplit` and `PaneBeside` keep a page each at every text size. Only a spread (`PaneSplit(isSpread: true)`: a practice's questions, onboarding's questions, the mock runner, an essay being written) becomes one column where its text stacks, and `PaneSplit` keeps that column to the page before the hinge. A result is no spread, so a practice's result takes `PracticeTaskLayout(isResult: true)`: a page each, the way on at the bottom of the trailing one. `PaneBeside(atHingeOnly: true)` adds a page only an open book shows: the Plan's goal box faces the goal's examples, while a phone and a tablet keep the box alone.
- A screen with one decision and nothing real to face (the sign-in gate, a status message or gate, a screen still loading, an intro, wait or note in a practice or a challenge, a call that can't go on or that dropped, a mock's steps: expired, time up, handed in, the break, the speaking part; the lesson's gates and waits, a quick explanation's wait, a challenge moved to another day, Today's and the Plan's own states) keeps to the page before the hinge with `.lonePage()`, its bottom bar included without `.keepsOffFold()`, and the page after it stays blank. A practice's or a challenge's takes `PracticeTaskLayout(content:actions:)` without `isResult`, which does this. A step inside a running spread that asks nothing, such as a practice's Finish or a duel's end, keeps the spread, its way on where each answer's was. Onboarding's `onboardingColumn()` uses it too where nothing is beside the step (a course's start); with an aside, the plan so far or how it works stays on the trailing page at every text size.
- Other one-column screens (a call's result) let their scrolling content cross the fold and move their bottom action and their text fields off it with `.keepsOffFold()` on the control inside its row. A `List` isn't measured again when the Duo folds or unfolds, so a fold read from the list itself keeps the pose the screen opened in; its rows are, so read the fold inside a row.
- Sheets, alerts and confirmation dialogs are the system's own. Flat, it centers them across the hinge; partially folded, it moves them onto one page. A custom `PresentationSizing` is held to the page sheet's width, so a sheet never spans both pages: the account sheet's hub and a settings page stay one column there. So settings stay a system sheet on the Duo: a settings page opened on its own (Memory from the buddy's day, a settings row in Search, a buddy's link to a settings page, "Change limit") opens the account sheet at that page with `AppSheet.account(_:)`, and back leads to the settings list. They don't face the list as on Android.
- A result (the lesson's end, a practice's, a duel's or a mock's result, the session's summary) reads the result (its title, its numbers and the day's moment) on the leading page, and what came of it and what's next on the trailing one, above the way on, at every text size. A mock's result keeps its areas and what it showed on the leading page whether or not there's anything to review, and the questions to review keep their place in their list through a fold.
- The catalog stays one column on the open Duo and lines up with the hinge, flat or folded, so folding moves nothing: its grid takes `PaneSpace.columnsAroundHinge` (the same number of columns on each side, the hinge as their gap, as Apple asks of grids whether or not the fold is active) at every text size, a list row per line of the grid, and the block under its title ends at the hinge with `listSectionMargins`.

## Pitfalls

- `.secondary` and `.tertiary` are under AA on white in light mode, and they aren't main's neutrals in any mode. Supporting text is `.mutedForeground` or `.faint`, which the generator's test keeps at AA on every surface.
- The system button styles (`.bordered`, `.borderedProminent`, `.borderless`) draw the system's look, and with the ink tint a prominent label turns white on white in dark mode. Content buttons take `.mainAction`, `.outline` or `.ghost`.
- `Form` stays inset-grouped whatever list style it gets, so a page of settings or fields is a `ZoonkList`.
- A plain list's rows paint the system background whatever is behind them. Painting the page's color behind a list bands between rows in dark mode, so `ZoonkList` paints nothing.
- The asset `AccentColor` reaches neither `Toggle` nor `UISwitch.appearance()` in SwiftUI. Tint toggles explicitly.
- `Text + Text` is deprecated since iOS 26. Interpolate styled texts into one `Text` instead, for example a number and its unit: `Text("\(Text(minutes, format: .number).monospacedDigit()) \(Text(unit).foregroundStyle(.mutedForeground))")` (an interpolated piece can't take `.typeStyle(_:)`).
- A fixed `.frame(height:)` around text breaks at accessibility sizes. Let text set the height, or use `@ScaledMetric`.

## Characters

`DesignSystem/Characters` ports main's rig (`packages/ui/src/components/_buddy`, `trickster.tsx`, their acts) point for point: main's SVG path data is read by `Path(svg:)`, each character draws into one `Canvas` as named parts, each posed around its own pivot, and acts are main's `BUDDY_ACTS`/`TRICKSTER_ACTS` as data (frames at 24 a second, main's curve tokens as `ActEase`). One canvas keeps them crisp at any size and cheap; a `TimelineView` runs only while an act plays, so a character on an everyday screen costs nothing.

- `BuddyCharacter(look:expression:crop:reaction:cue:)`: `BuddyLook` (kind, glasses, belt, Energy) from `LearnerSummary.buddy` grows it and picks its everyday face. `TricksterCharacter(beaten:reacts:cue:)`. `BuddyFace` is the face at text size, for labels and pickers.
- A `reaction` plays once when the view appears, where the learner's work just did something (pass none on a revisit, and the landing expression instead); a `cue` plays on each change of its id (a duel's verdict). Reduce Motion lands the act's expression at once.
- Their colors are artwork (`Color(art:)`), the same in light and dark as main draws them; the belt takes `ProgressBelt.color`. They're decorative: `accessibilityHidden`, with the moment's words carrying the meaning.
- `Burst` flies the rarest moments' sparks once (a belt, the buddy's next stage). `MilestoneStage` is a ceremony's picture.
- See every drawing and act in a Debug build launched with `ZOONK_DEBUG_CHARACTERS=1` (`=ceremony` for a milestone's ceremony), the native side of main's `/design/characters`.
