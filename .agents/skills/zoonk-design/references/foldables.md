# Book foldables

An unfolded book-style foldable (iPhone Duo, Galaxy Z Fold, Pixel Fold) is nearly square, but it reads like an open book or wallet with two sides. Zoonk uses it the same way on every platform: a page on each side of the fold, each about a phone wide, so the phone's views go in unchanged. Never one phone column stretched across the fold, and never a tablet layout. This is the product rule; each platform's mapping says how it's built: Apple's [iPhone Duo](../../apple-human-interface-guidelines/references/design-system.md#iphone-duo), Android's [foldables](../../android-material-guidelines/references/design-system.md#foldables).

- **Folded, a phone.** The outer display is a phone's screen: one column, the phone's navigation.
- **Open, two pages that don't move.** The panes meet at the fold whether the device lies flat or stands half folded like a book, so folding it moves nothing. Decide one or two panes by the space the screen has, never by the device.
- **Nothing on the fold.** Text, buttons and fields stay off it, and so do the navigation's items. Scrolling content of a one-column screen may cross it; that screen's bottom action and its fields keep off it.
- **Every feature in every pose.** Nothing hides behind a pose or the hinge's angle. Held on its side and half folded (tabletop), content sits on the upright half and controls on the flat one, which bottom bars already do.
- **The learner's place survives.** Folding, unfolding and rotating keep answers, drafts, the selection, what's open and the scroll position.
- **Too narrow, one column.** Where a side would be narrower than a phone's column, the screen stays one column. At a fold, large text alone doesn't merge the pages: the fold sets each page's width, so a page and what supports it keep a page each and their text reflows inside them, as on a phone. A spread becomes one column where its text is too large for its pages, and that column keeps to the page before the fold.

## What goes on each side

| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Spread: lesson player, practice, placement and level-test questions, the mock runner, a call's intro                                                 | the screen: question or title, teaching, stimulus, worked example, what the call is                                                                  | the learner's turn: options, fields, canvas, feedback, the call's goals, with the action bar at its bottom                                                                                               |
| Main and supporting: Today, Plan, Buddy with or without a goal, Exam, Search, the mistakes notebook, a plan's subject, chapter or unit, onboarding   | the main content: the day or the goal box, the buddy, Search's actions or a query's matches, the mistakes to fix, an onboarding step as on the phone | what supports it: missions, the week, numbers, settings, the buddy's day, the goal's examples, the places to go or the catalog, the fixed mistakes, the plan so far (how it works before there's a goal) |
| List and detail: Statistics, settings, a settings page opened on its own, a mock's chooser                                                           | the list, such as the mocks to pick                                                                                                                  | the selected item, such as the picked mock with the chooser's Start at its bottom                                                                                                                        |
| Landing: a course's or a chapter's page                                                                                                              | the course or chapter and its way in (a course's example plan, a chapter's lessons)                                                                  | what it covers: who it's for, the outline, what you'll learn, the way in again                                                                                                                           |
| Result: the lesson's end, a practice's or a mock's intro and result, an essay's grade, a milestone, the session's next stop and summary, the logbook | what was done: the result or the intro, the milestone's stage, the missions, the week                                                                | its numbers, what's next (a milestone's words, a mock's questions to review) and the way on                                                                                                              |

- An action keeps the form it has on the phone and stays with what it acts on. A button under its answer stays under it, as onboarding's Continue does on the leading page. A bar pinned to the bottom of the phone's screen goes to the bottom of the page holding what it acts on: the player's answer, the mock chooser's picked mock.
- In the lesson player, Previous and the main action stay at the bottom of the trailing page on every screen. The leading page is what the learner reads and looks at, the trailing page what they answer with and what the answer gets back, and each page scrolls on its own only when it runs out of room:
  - An activity whose check asks a question about its canvas puts the canvas under its prompt on the leading page, with the question, its options or number field and the verdict on the trailing page. An interaction's canvas is the answer, so it stays on the trailing page.
  - A question screen (options, a field or an interaction) ends its leading page with "From the lesson", folded, where the question has one: the explanation it sits next to in the lesson, read beside the answer. Open, it stays open through a fold; in one column it's under the question and its verdict.
  - A worked example keeps its problem and the problem's picture or chart on the leading page; its steps unfold on the trailing page, right above the button that shows the next one.
  - An explanation or a hook's text puts its picture, chart or something to explore on the trailing page, which then ends with help with the screen where the learner can ask the buddy: two of the buddy's suggestions for it and "Ask something else", in place of the offer that comes after a long pause. A word card puts the word and its sound on the leading page and what it means on the trailing page. A letter card and a summary stay on the leading page.
  - No text flows across the fold.
- What the learner did on a canvas lives next to the answer and is kept, shown and cleared exactly when the answer is, so folding, unfolding or rotating never resets it, and Previous onto an answered screen shows it with the result. A canvas made again opens where the learner left it, or at its own starting values; only a checked screen may open where the check's expected answer was worked out.
- A side panel (the lesson's Ask, the buddy's day) is the trailing pane where the screen splits. Where the screen is one column, the panel is a sheet, shown only when it was opened there, so closing the device never covers the screen with one.
- A pane is about a phone wide even on a wide window: what's inside it lays out as it does on a phone.
- A mock's result puts what happened before the fold (the score, the answer counts, the areas, what the mock showed, the day's moment) and what's next after it (the plan change, the mistakes and questions to review, the mock's notes, then the actions). With nothing to review, the areas and what the mock showed go after the fold too, so neither page sits empty.
- A screen with one decision (sign-in, a status message or gate, Today's and the Plan's own states) takes the page before the fold, and the other page stays blank: a blank page reads better than filler. A one-column screen that has something real to face faces it: a goal box the goal's examples, Buddy without a goal the buddy's day, a settings page opened on its own the settings list.
- One-column screens (a call's result) let their scrolling content cross the fold and keep their bottom action and text fields off it. Onboarding's one column keeps to the page before the fold instead, as on a course's start, which main shows with nothing beside it, and on a placement or level-test question whose text is too large to spread.
- Dialogs (alerts, confirmations, date and time pickers) open on the page after the fold and stay within it, as sheets do; on a tabletop, on the flat half. Each is the platform's own, moved, never a custom one. Snackbars show over the actions they sit with, never across the fold.
- A grid lines up with the fold, flat or folded, so folding moves nothing: the same number of columns on each side, the fold as their gap.
