# App patterns

Concrete patterns of the learning app (`packages/learn`, `apps/main`). Reuse them before inventing a new one; extend the shared piece when a screen needs more. Code identifiers may still say `journey` and `tutor`; what learners see says Plan and buddy.

## One frame

- Every app page renders inside one frame, `apps/main/src/app/[lang]/(app)/layout.tsx` (`LearnFrame`), for learners, guests and visitors alike: the tabs and the pages they open, the Mistakes notebook, the exam and its mock chooser, mind maps, speaking, statistics, settings, the catalog (its course, chapter and lesson pages, `(catalog)/b/**`, included) and onboarding (`/start`, a new goal's questions, a course's start and the placement mock's chooser, `(onboarding)/**`). Navigating swaps only the page; the sidebar, the bars and the tab bar stay mounted. The one exception is the player: the screens where the learner does the work (a lesson, a session, a running mock, a challenge, a checkpoint, an essay, a test-out, mistakes practice, a call, a pattern, pronunciation, the focus test, an explanation, the logbook's weekly recap) are full screen. A page that chooses or sets up the work (onboarding, a mock's chooser) is an app page, not a task. Public marketing pages (the landing, pricing, a shared plan link, the legal pages) keep the public layout.
- The router keeps recently visited pages hidden in the document, so nothing in the frame may depend on which page is inside it: the main's padding never changes per page (`PageWithAside` reaches past it with negative margins), and no frame style reads page content through `:has()`. The one exception is `TabRootOnly`'s first-paint copy of the top bar, hidden on a page with its own bar; it exists only before the router keeps any other page.
- Widths. A phone's page has 16px gutters. From `sm` to `lg` (a tablet in portrait) the page and its bars are one centered 48rem column; `BAR_ROW_CLASS` gives every bar the page's edges. From `lg` the sidebar (240px) sits on the left and the page starts 40px from it: its title, its tabs, its bar's breadcrumb and its content share that one left edge on every page, and the page fills the room beside the sidebar (or up to the aside) with a 40px gutter on the right too (`PAGE_COLUMN_CLASS`, which `Page`, `DetailLayout` and `SectionPage` carry). A page that's worked in never centers inside the frame, so a wide screen never adds a margin on the left; extra width goes to the content (onboarding's screens, which ask one thing at a time, are the one exception, below):
  - **Grids gain columns**: Explore courses lays its cards out with `repeat(auto-fill, minmax(14rem, 1fr))` (two columns at 1024 to 1180px, three at 1280 to 1440, five at 1920, eight at 2560), and the mind maps' gallery adds columns the same way.
  - **Lists, roadmaps, charts and dashboards grow**: Today's queue, the Plan's roadmap and subjects, a subject's chapters, the exam and statistics span the room, as Linear's lists and Apple's web apps do.
  - **The aside** holds what supports the page from 72rem (below).
  - **What has to stay readable keeps its measure on the same left edge**: prose, a stat's explanation and a notebook entry keep the 40rem column (`max-w-column`, as wide as a question in the player); and the notebook's inbox (its 20rem skill list, then the selected skill's entries at that measure) ends with them, its header and "Practice mistakes" included (`INBOX_PAGE_CLASS`), so the main action never sits past the last word; the settings forms, Plus inside the app and the buddy's conversation keep the form measure (`max-w-form`, 45rem). A section's header and tabs (statistics, settings) still span the room over a hairline, so a narrow form sits under a full-width header rather than floating as a column.
  - **A flow that asks one thing at a time centers it**: an onboarding screen keeps the 40rem column centered in the page between the sidebar and the aside, under the page's bar, as the player's questions sit under its header (see [Tasks and steps](#tasks-and-steps)). It has nothing to fill the room with but the question, its answer and its next step, which read down one column, so even room on both sides reads as focus, while the column on the page's left edge read as a strip beside missing content, and a question beside its answer split one thought across the screen (Typeform, Duolingo and Google's sign-up keep one centered column; digital exams split only a long passage from its question).
  - Measured in October 2026 at 1440, 1920 and 2560: Apple Podcasts, Apple Music and the App Store on the web start their content 40px from a 260px sidebar and fill the room (App Store caps its content near 1,600px, centering past it); Khan Academy's course page starts 48px from its unit list and fills; Twitch's directory starts about 24px from its sidebar and grows its grid from five columns to nine. Centered columns showed up on docs, marketing and channel pages (Stripe's, Vercel's and Linear's docs, GitHub, a YouTube channel), which are read like documents rather than worked in.
- `PageWithAside`, from 72rem: the page fills the room between the sidebar and the aside, which runs the screen's full height at its right edge behind a hairline, 300px (320px from 80rem), scrolling on its own. Under 72rem its blocks follow the page in the same column (`asideClassName` lays them out, such as two up on a tablet). The aside holds supporting status and properties, never a second main column: Today's missions, week, preparation and notice; the Plan's numbers, suggestion, next steps and settings; a detail page's properties; a public lesson's chapter; the buddy's day.

## Navigation

- **The sidebar, from `lg`** (`LearnAppSidebar`): the goal switcher and "Search or ask…" (its ⌘K or Ctrl K keycap on pointer devices) on top; the three tabs (Today, ending with what's left of the day in mono; Plan; the buddy, by its name and its face wearing today's missions as a ring); the "Study" group: Mistakes notebook (its open count in mono), Exam (an exam goal; marked on its mock chooser too), Speaking (a language goal with a speaking paper), Mind maps (once the goal has some), Statistics, then Explore courses, which every learner and visitor has, marked on `/courses`, its categories and `/b/…`. The account sits at its end as one row that opens the account menu (above it, from its start): the 24px avatar, the learner's name ("Guest" for a guest) and their belt and level, the row 44px tall and named by all three ("Ana Souza, Yellow belt, level 4, User menu"); Statistics is in the Study group and the menu. A visitor gets "Pricing" (a row) and "Log in" there instead, and the logo, going home, in the goal switcher's place. Rows are 32px at 13.5px, each 44px to press. The current item sits on a soft fill as a whole (icon and label), and a page with its own sidebar link marks that link, not a tab. Every icon is the same neutral color, the shortcuts to a kind's page (the notebook, the exam, speaking, mind maps) included: kind hues live in the content.
- **Phones and tablets** (under `lg`): a tab's own page (Today, Plan, the buddy) has the top bar: the goal switcher on the left (its mark, name and chevron, no box), the belt and level (opening Statistics) and the 28px avatar on the right, and the "Search or ask…" field across under them; a visitor's bar has the logo (home) on the left and "Pricing" and "Log in" on the right. The bottom tab bar stays on every app page but onboarding's, the page's tab current: the Plan for a subject, a chapter, a unit, the exam (and its mock chooser) and mind maps; Today for the notebook and speaking; none for statistics, settings and the catalog, which belong to no tab. Onboarding has no tab bar: until the plan exists the tabs would lead to a half-made one, and its bar has the way back and out. It takes its own room at the end of the page, clears the home indicator, and toasts rise above it.
- **Pushed pages** (a subject, a chapter, a unit, a catalog course, chapter or lesson): `LearnPageBar` takes the top bar's place under `lg` and sits over the page beside the sidebar from `lg`. The way back is on its left: under `lg` a 44px chevron named "Back to {page}", joined by the page's name once its `h1` scrolls under the bar; from `lg` a breadcrumb with the parent and the page's own name ("‹ Plan / Linguagens"). The page's actions sit on its right (a chapter's "Ask", a subject's under 72rem; a catalog page's "…" and, under `lg`, a visitor's "Log in"). The bar keeps one height with or without actions and draws its hairline once the page scrolls. Back returns to the previous page as it was (scroll and all) when that's where the learner came from, and to the page's parent otherwise; Escape presses it.
- **Sidebar roots** (the Mistakes notebook, the exam, mind maps, speaking) are places the sidebar lists, so `LearnPageBar root` draws no breadcrumb from `lg` (and no bar at all without actions) and their titles sit where Today's and the Plan's do. Under `lg` a bare chevron ("Back"), and Escape at every size, return to the app page the learner came from, whichever it was (`goBackToApp`, which skips the page's own tabs), or to its default page.
- **Sections** (statistics, settings) use `SectionPage`. From `md` the section owns the header: a quiet eyebrow (whose account it is, "All goals"), the section's name where every title sits, and its pages as tabs under it (`SectionNav`) over a hairline, so moving between them swaps only what's under the tabs; each page keeps its own `h1` for screen readers (`SectionPageHeader`). Under `md` the section's bar (`LearnSectionBar`) is the page's top: the hub (`/settings`, a list of the settings pages; `/stats`, the overview) leaves the section for the app page the learner left for it, each page under the hub goes back to the hub with its name beside the chevron (or to the app page it was opened from, such as Plus from a locked feature), and Escape presses it.
- **Explore courses**, the catalog's hub, has its own bar under `lg` (`CatalogBar`): the way back to where the learner came from (Today, or the home page for a visitor) and the account menu or "Log in". The category chips stay in reach under it; from `lg` the categories are a sticky column beside the courses, each with its course count in mono.
- **Search and commands.** ⌘K/Ctrl+K opens the command palette on any page; the search field, the sidebar's "Search or ask…" and the account menu's Search open the same palette. It goes to pages (Go to), runs actions (continue today's session, start a new goal, take a mock exam), finds the learner's goals, plan chapters and finished mocks and the catalog ("In the catalog"), and asks the buddy (Ask). A visitor's palette stays short: Go to, Explore courses, Log in, and the settings pages once they search.
- **The account menu**: whose account it is (photo, name, email, and "Plus" when that's the plan), Search, Statistics (with the level) and Explore courses, Settings and Help, then the way out: "Logout" for an account; "Create an account" and "Log in" for a guest, whose session isn't an account yet (logging out would lose the plan). A visitor has no account menu: the bars and the sidebar show "Log in", and the palette holds the rest.
- **The goal switcher** only switches and starts: each goal with its mark (a language's flag, or its first letter on the goal's color) over its time a day and days left (or "Paused", "Finished") in mono, then the quick explanations not read to the end (each opens itself; read to the end, one leaves the menu and search finds it), "Start a new goal" and "Explore courses". While a switch is on its way the page dims and is out of reach. What acts on one goal (pause, resume, archive) lives in its Plan's "…". A learner starts a goal, never adds one.
- No logo inside the app for anyone with a session. A visitor's frame shows the logo, going home, where the goal switcher would be. Logos otherwise belong to marketing pages and the login page.

## Page templates

Every page is one of these, built from the kit in `packages/learn/src/_components/` (`page.tsx`, `detail-page.tsx`, `list-group.tsx`) and the shell's `PageWithAside`, so learners always see where they are, what's on the page and where its main action is.

- **Tab root and section hub** (Today, Plan, the buddy; Settings, Statistics): `Page` > `PageHeader` with the page's name as its title (`PageTitle`, `text-title`), a mono eyebrow for its context (`PageEyebrow`: the date and where the plan stands; the goal and its date) and at most one supporting line (`PageSubtitle`). Beside the title at most its main action and one "…".
  - **Today** has one job, starting or continuing the day: its title with Start or Continue beside it from `sm` (under the queue on a phone), the day's segments, the queue with the next block highlighted (its kind's icon, `KindMark`, or the Trickster's face for a phase's challenge; the title and subject; mono minutes; a done row is one quiet line: the done mark in its icon's place, its name muted and never struck, its minutes); every block still to do opens from its row in any order, the whole row one target, while Continue keeps the day's order; a phase's challenge with lessons still before it says it waits for them instead of opening, and done rows open nothing, and one line saying what the day is in every other state (done, a rest day, nothing new, lessons being written). The aside holds the week's rings, the goal's number drawn in its own units (an exam's preparation over its subjects as the Plan lists them, each one the session still studies today tagged "Today"; another goal's progress over its lessons as cells, one each, today's still to do outlined in blue; a language's level on the CEFR ladder, the half steps up to its target outlined), the whole card one link to the Plan named by its words, and the one notice; today's missions lead the aside as one card (`MissionsCard`: the buddy offers them, what's left and about how long, the reward in Brain Power's violet over a track that fills, each mission opening where it's done; one line once all are done or when another goal already earned the bonus), and the queue row each open mission opens wears a small violet target beside its minutes; under 72rem they follow the day, the notice first as one line, then the missions card (their one place on a phone too: a second line about them under the day's bar repeated the card on the same screen), then "Practice anytime", which only phones and tablets show (from 72rem the sidebar and the palette hold those ways in). A learner or visitor without a goal gets the start form in Today's place, its examples and "How it works" in the aside.
  - **Plan**: its title with "Adjust plan" and the goal's "…", the roadmap (phases as bands with their dates from `sm`; on a phone a compact bar that opens the phases sheet), then the subjects (an exam's or a course's modules) or the path for goals without subjects. Its aside holds the two numbers (the goal's measure: preparation, progress or level; and the pace), the suggestion slot, "Next on your path" and "Your plan", each setting a row that opens the editor at it. Under 72rem the numbers are tiles and the suggestion a line after the roadmap, the rest follows the page (two up on a tablet), and the settings live in "Adjust plan" (a side sheet from `md`, a bottom drawer on phones). Under `lg`, without a sidebar, the exam's page and the mind maps close the page as rows.
- **Detail page** (a subject, a chapter, a unit, the exam, the Mistakes notebook), as Linear's issue view: `DetailLayout` with its bar, then `DetailHero`: `DetailEyebrow` naming what it belongs to, `DetailTitle`, and `DetailFacts`, one mono line of facts with the progress in it ("4 lessons · 26 min · 50% done"). `DetailActions` holds the one main action ("Continue" or "Start", which Enter follows) and the outline "…" as tall as it (`DetailMenu`, or `ContentVoteMenu size="detail"`), beside the name from `md` and full width under it on a phone; progress never goes inside the button. The sections follow flat on the page, and the page's properties (`DetailAsideSection`, `DetailProperties`: coverage, details, practice, "Also") sit in the aside from 72rem and after the sections under it. The exam's main action is "Take a mock exam" ("Continue your mock exam" while one runs); the notebook's is "Practice mistakes".
- **Catalog page** (a course, a chapter, a public lesson): written for a visitor from a search who doesn't know Zoonk yet, so each page says what it is, what it makes someone able to do, what Zoonk does with it and the one way in, in that order, and a course's page starts teaching before it explains anything. `CatalogPageBar`, then `Page` as a stack of bands across the page (`BAND_CLASS` in `subject-tone.ts`: to the screen's edges on a phone, a rounded panel of the column on a tablet, from the sidebar to the window's edge from `lg`, the content on the page's own edges), so where one part ends and the next starts shows at a glance. Parts read apart by neutral tone steps (the page and `ground`), never a wash of the subject's color: the course's subject shows only as its label (`SubjectLabel`) and its line (`bg-subject` under `data-subject`, see [color](color.md)).
  - **First screen**: the name (the course's mark over a course's, and over an exam course's name its next sitting as a mono eyebrow, "Exam on Nov 8 and Nov 15 · 25 days left"; the course as an eyebrow over a chapter's or a lesson's), its promise in `max-w-column`, one quieter line on the plan after the Plan's map ("With Zoonk, your plan starts at your level and fits your schedule.", or "at this chapter" from a chapter that starts there) and **Build my plan** with "Start free, no account needed." under it for visitors. Beside it from `xl`, at the page's right edge, a course shows the first written guess question among its first lessons ("Try a lesson · free · 5 min", `TryLesson`), answerable in place (an answer opens the player, which closes back to the page it was tried on); under `xl` the question follows the way in, and an outline "Try a lesson" beside Build my plan leads down to it (from `xl`, where it's in sight, there's none). Without a question, the example plan takes its place beside the course. A lesson's first screen is its first question instead, the page's main action.
  - **A course's example plan** (`CoursePlanWindow`, its own band after the first screen, or beside the course when there's no question): "Example plan" and what core's planner makes of the course at the example's time a day (`estimateCoursePlan`, no AI; 15 minutes, 30 for an exam): the weeks to its end from scratch and, for an end more than six weeks out, the month it ends for someone starting today (core's `endDate`, the end the Plan's road shows), with Today's first lessons beside it; or for an exam course read against its next sitting, the time it studies by then, the lessons that fit and the share of its topics. Its numbers come only from core; with no estimate there's no window. Under the number, "What fills the time", its whole beside the label, lists the plan's lessons, lessons not written yet, reviews and practice, and challenges and mocks (core's `workload`), each after its kind's dot as Today marks it, so the weeks agree with the outline's hours, and one line says what the reviews are for. The window lays itself out by its room: one column beside the course or on a phone, two from 48rem. The countdown counts in the exam's time zone; it's cached until midnight, so a prefetch carries it, and a caption says it's an example. "Build my plan" carries the example's time a day to the start (`?minutes=`), and Pricing's "Get Plus" its billing period (`/subscription?period=`).
  - **Fit** (`CourseFit`): who it's for, then an arrow to what you'll be able to do, in the course's own words read as where the visitor starts and where they get to, side by side from `lg`; what you'll be able to do sits on a panel. Both lists are short enough to take in at a glance (three people, four abilities, a few words each), so nothing folds.
  - **Outline**: folded by level, the first level open, its size as a mono line on the section ("36 chapters · 213 short lessons"), every chapter and lesson link once in the server HTML; a level or an area of more than eight chapters shows its first six and the rest one tap away (`OutlineChapterList`, through `ShowMore`: a native disclosure whose toggle sits after what it opens, in the page's HTML all along), and an open level's row drops the chapter names its rows repeat. Each chapter says its week in the example plan. An exam course's outline goes by the exam's areas instead, each folded to how much of it fits, its chapters in the plan's order and marked with their week or "Left out". The closing band ends the page with the way in again.
  - **A chapter**: where it sits (`ChapterStrip`, the course's chapters as segments by level), the next three chapters, its lessons as numbered rows beside a question to try and what you'll learn, then "Your plan from this chapter", its first screen and closing in its course's bands. **A lesson**: under its question, one example day of the plan with the lesson between its chapter's neighbors ("This lesson is part of the … course.") and an outline "Build my plan".
  - A course and a chapter fill the room like a detail page, their text in the column measure: the question to try (or the plan) sits at the page's right edge, and the plan's band, the outline's levels, a chapter's lessons and its strip grow; a lesson keeps `PageWithAside` with its chapter's lessons beside it from 72rem (folded under the question below).
  - "Build my plan" is a link to the course's start page, whose own button creates the plan, so nothing starts from a catalog page. From a course, the course's first chapter, a chapter whose lessons aren't written yet and a lesson of the first chapter, the start page asks the visitor's level for the whole course; from any later chapter with lessons, and a lesson in one, it shows the plan from that chapter on, as the end of a guest's lesson does.
  - These pages are indexed: one H1 (the name, as in the title), their metadata, canonical URLs and structured data stay.
- **Section page** (a settings page, a stat's page): the section's header and tabs from `md`, the page's own title and line on a phone. The section's header and tabs span the room; settings are forms in the form measure (45rem) under them, on the same left edge; a stat's page is a dashboard that fills the room and shows its numbers (`StatTiles`) and its chart.
- **Browse** (Explore courses): the title and its one line, the goal box for a goal no course covers (the landing's, "Build my plan"), the categories that have courses, then the courses: one flat list with hairlines under `lg` (a 40px picture or a flag, the title, its description and a mono "23 lessons · about 2 hr"), cards from `lg` that fill the room and gain columns with it (at least 14rem each). An empty category isn't listed; its page, reached from an old link, offers "Start a goal".
- **Public page** (the landing, pricing): the public layout (the bar with Courses and Pricing, the footer with popular courses and the categories that have courses), the page's promise as its one H1, then stops (`Stop`: a mono eyebrow with its mark, a one-sentence title, one lead line), each showing the product in its window (`ProductWindow`), drawn from the app's own pieces (list rows, `KindDot`, the roadmap's band styles in `PlanRoad`, the answer tones) and never copies of them. Example data says it's an example; its dates are written as of one day and move with the calendar (cached until midnight), an exam example counting to the exam's real next sitting, so no example date is ever in the past. The footer offers every language by its own name. A choice a visitor makes to see the product respond (an example goal, yesterday studied or not, a billing period, a time a day) is a `VariantSwitch`: native radios and CSS, every variant in the HTML, no client code, its name unique per page.
- **Task** (lessons, sessions, mocks, challenges, results): `TaskFrame`, below.
- **Conversation** (the buddy tab): one compact row on top (the buddy's face, name and stage, and its "…"), then the conversation, its suggestions and composer always in reach. Under 72rem the buddy's day (Energy and missions) is one quiet line that opens its sheet; from 72rem it sits in the aside (`BuddyDay`). The buddy's words are plain text on the page; only the learner's sit in a bubble. With a mouse or trackpad the composer takes focus on open; on a phone it waits for a tap, so the keyboard doesn't cover the conversation.

## What every page shares

- **Titles at one height.** `PageHeaderContent` keeps the eyebrow's line even when it's empty, and a section's header keeps its own, so moving between pages never moves the title. Gates, empty states and skeletons keep the page's header too.
- **Sections**: every block of content sits under a `PageSection` header (`PageSectionTitle`, a sentence-case title at the body's size, never an uppercase label, with a quiet mono count or state on the right in `PageSectionDetail`: "7 lessons", "3 of 9"). Groups inside a section are named by `PageSectionLabel` (13px, sentence case: "Objective tests", "Physics"); sources and rules go in a `PageSectionFooter` under the content. `SectionLabel` is only for small groups inside a sheet, a step or a folded panel.
- **One list**: `ListGroup`, flat rows with a hairline between them, as wide as the column (`variant="panel"` only for a self-contained list, such as one inside a sheet). Rows are `ListRow`, `ListRowLink` (the whole row a link, with its chevron), `ListRowButton`, or a native trigger with `LIST_ROW_INTERACTIVE_CLASS`: a leading mark (`ListRowLeading`, `ListRowIcon`: a quiet 16px glyph, or a `KindDot`), `ListRowContent` > `ListRowTitle` (14px) and `ListRowDescription` (12px), and `ListRowTrailing` in mono. The current row (`aria-current`, or `CURRENT_ROW_CLASS`), the hover and the focus ring draw on a highlight a little past the column's edges, so rows stay square and the hairlines run straight. A highlighted row (current, picked, or where an outline starts) drops the hairlines touching it, above and below, so its fill reads as one shape: every divided list draws its hairlines with `divided-rows` (`@zoonk/ui` styles, inside `ListGroup`), which turns them transparent around the item marked `aria-current`, or `data-highlighted` when it isn't the current one, so nothing moves.
- **One status vocabulary**: `StatusMark` (a blue check once done, a muted fast-forward when skipped because the learner showed they know it, the learner's dot with a halo, in the text color, for the next one, its row highlighted in the neutral fill, a faint dot ahead) and `ChapterMark` (adds a small ring for one begun), the same for chapters, lessons, topics and phases; a list of one kind (the exam's mocks, the notebook's skills) marks its rows this way rather than with a kind's dot. Choices to pick are rows of one list with a ring at their end, filled in the text color with a check once picked, the picked row on the pick's tint (`ChoiceList`); a question's answers are `AnswerOption`s, a pick ringed in the text color until it's checked, then blue when right or pink-red when wrong.
- **Surfaces and notices**: content sits flat on the page; a `Surface` (`SURFACE_CLASS`) holds one self-contained thing, never another panel. A note a page wants read is a `NoticeCard` (`NoticeCardLeading`, `NoticeCardContent` > `NoticeCardTitle`, `NoticeCardDescription`, `NoticeCardActions`: a small primary and a secondary); a task's one rule is a `Callout`.
- **One suggestion at a time** (`Suggestion`): the one thing a page wants the learner to know or decide. Under 72rem it's a line under the page's work (its mark, one sentence, and "View" or its own action), and "View" opens it whole in a bottom sheet; from 72rem it's a panel in the aside, answered in place. Once answered, the line and the panel say what the answer did, with Undo while it can be undone. The Plan's slot keeps its items in order (paused, each exam choice, past its date, finished, too tight, behind, coverage, free week over; a finished plan whose time left part of the goal out offers the coverage before "Continue at …", as a unit is finished before the next one) and pages them with "‹ 1 of 3 ›" (`SlotPager`): in the panel, and under the line on phones, so a line that acts on its own ("Adjust", "Resume") still leads to the next; the sheet holds one suggestion and no pager. Today shows the newest waiting proposal, and "Next suggestion" brings up the next once it's answered. Apply shows only once the change's effect is computed (a −/+ diff first); a preview that fails says "We couldn't check what changes." with Try again and Not now. Every plan diff (`PlanEffectDiff`) leads with the biggest change a learner can see, the lessons it leaves out or adds, then the goal's date and the plan's end, then phases, areas, the next mock and the week's event day; a day never breaks across lines, and a wrapped line hangs under its words, not under its sign. Beside a diff, words say only what it can't (the lessons left out by name, the topics).
- **Calm failures.** Something that failed says so plainly and offers the way on: a `NoticeCard` with a muted icon, the title in the text color, a muted line and "Try again" (`GenerationFailureNotice`), or, for a page that didn't load, `PageErrorMessage` (`FramePageError` inside the frame, which keeps its bars). Pink-red text is only for a wrong answer and a one-line inline error.
- **Status lines never push the page.** A slow or failed action's message ("Opening your next step…", "We couldn't open the next step. Try again.") takes the place of a line that's already there, such as the day's line under Today's button, so the title and the segments never move. A notice that arrives on its own while someone is answering goes below what they're tapping, never above it: onboarding's "Building your plan stopped" sits with Continue in the questions' footer, its Try again beside the next step. Live regions stay rendered while empty.
- **One main action per page, always in the same place**: beside the title on Today (from `sm`) and on a detail page (from `md`), full width under the content on a phone, and pinned at the bottom on a task. In a row of a page's buttons the main action comes first, at every size ("Resume | New goal", "Apply | Not now"), matching the reading, focus and screen-reader order. Modal dialogs (`dialog.tsx`, `alert-dialog.tsx`) keep the platform order: the main action last on the right, on top when the buttons stack on a phone. Secondary actions are outline or ghost buttons, never underlined links outside running text.
- **44px targets.** Everything tappable is at least 44px to press. `Button`, the sidebar's rows and the kit's links carry `hit-area` (a centered 44px `::before`; the element must be positioned and must not clip its overflow); inputs are 44px tall, since an input can't take a hit area; rows that touch each other are 44px tall instead, since a hit area would reach into the next. The E2E accessibility scans check every target's size.
- **Mono for data, readouts for the number that matters.** Geist Mono with tabular figures for durations, counts, scores, timers, dates and percentages in rows and lines made of them: the eyebrow, a facts line, a row's trailing value. A block's one big number is a readout in the text face with tabular figures, its unit small and muted (`BigQuantity`, `text-readout`), with a meter of parts under it when the number has parts (`MeterParts`). A value in words ("No date", "To be confirmed", "Everything", "weekends", a month inside a sentence: "you'd finish around February 2027") stays in the text face even where its numbers are mono (`t.rich` with a `<mono>` tag around the numbers).
- **Skeletons keep their real words.** A page's skeleton is its loaded layout's shape, with its static words drawn for real (the page's title, section labels such as "This week") and bars only for what varies (dates, names, counts), so loading to loaded moves nothing. A skeleton in `apps/main` reads main's catalog while the page it stands for may be drawn by `@zoonk/learn` from learn's: keep a word only where both catalogs translate it the same, otherwise draw a bar or move the skeleton into learn.
- **Prefetch fully where the URL matters.** Links to app pages prefetch in full (`LearnLink` and `ListRowLink` do by default; main's sidebar, menu and section links pass `prefetch`), so a page whose content depends on its URL (a chapter, a subject, a notebook tab, a palette result, a catalog course) paints at once on a click. Only a destination that does something when it opens opts out: practice, which picks a fresh set of mistakes (`prefetch={false}`), and a lesson that may still need writing (`prefetch="auto"`). Pages never use `export const instant = false`; `apps/main/e2e/instant-navigation.test.ts` locks instant navigation in with `instant()`.
- **Gates under the page's header.** A page that needs an account or a goal keeps its own header (its title and one line on what the page is) and shows the gate right under it: `AuthGatePage` (log in and come back to this page, or start free; a guest creates an account) or `LearnNoGoal` (start a goal, on Today). Task screens outside the frame use `AuthGateScreen`. Nothing redirects on the session; a browser whose session ended is told on Today: "Your session ended. Log in to pick up your plan."
- A visual anchor on every page (the buddy or the Trickster, a big number, a ring); three levels of type in a block at most: the main thing, its supporting line, its meta.

## Plus and limits

- **Nothing is hidden behind Plus.** A free learner or a guest sees every feature a Plus learner sees, in the same place and the same look, locked: its row, card, path entry or button carries `PlusMark` (a quiet 22px "Plus" chip; screen readers hear "Available with Plus") and still opens where it would. There the feature shows as it is (a mock's lengths, a challenge's intro, the buddy's card) with the one `PlusNotice` ("Available with Plus", what Plus unlocks in one line, "See Plus").
- A feature's own home carries the notice (the exam page's mocks, the mock chooser, a mock's intro, the buddy's card); lists, timelines and Today carry only the mark, so a learner who opens the app every day isn't asked every day. At most one notice on a screen, inline, never a dialog, a toast or a repeated prompt. On a task screen "See Plus" is the main action and the notice drops its own link (`cta={false}`); inside something that already has a panel it's `inset`.
- What the free plan had and ended (an exam plan's free first week) uses the same notice with its own title, once, where the learner meets it.
- A cap reached is not a locked feature: lessons, help and calls say until when and the one thing to do (`LessonLimitNotice`, `HelpLimitNotice`, `CallLimitNotice`: a guest creates a free account, a free learner sees Plus, Plus waits). The notice takes the place of what the cap stops (the buddy's composer shows it instead of a field that looks usable). No plan, price page or limit message shows how many minutes of calls a plan has; they say that Plus has higher call limits.

## Tasks and steps

- Every focused task uses `TaskFrame` (the lesson player shares its parts): the ✕ in the window's top-left corner (asking first when leaving would drop unsent work), the title centered over the column (an essay's is its rubric, "Essay · ENEM rubric"), an action or "⋯" in the top-right corner (just left of the aside when there is one; never the buddy, which has its own place below) or nothing, and the progress under the title: one segment per graded screen, blue when right and pink-red when wrong (`TaskHeaderSegments`), one thin bar otherwise, or a bar of the task's own, such as the Trickster's shield. What scrolls under the header fades out at its edge, and once the page scrolls the header draws its hairline across the task's room, as the app's bars do. The content starts at the top of the 40rem column (`max-w-column`, `TASK_COLUMN_CLASS`), and one action bar is pinned at the bottom over a hairline across the task's room at every size, so the main action (and Enter) never moves between screens. The main action fills the bar on a phone and takes its label's width from `lg` (`TaskMainButton`), where the bar's row fills the task's room, padded like the header's: the main action in its bottom-right corner under ⋯, Back in its bottom-left under ✕, so no button floats mid-bar, a quiet line about an action (a save error, a wait) at the right beside it, and a call's controls at the column's measure under its transcript; on a phone a long secondary that would squeeze it beside the buddy's icon sits on its own quiet row under it (a mock result's "See the 13 questions" and "Not now"); quiet actions in the column ("Explain first", "I don't know yet") keep the column's text edge, their hover reaching past it. The buddy sits in the same place on every task and every state: an icon after the main action below `lg`, saying its name there once a miss makes asking worth it, and from `lg` at the aside's foot, level with the action bar, as the composer (or, on a mock's result, a button), by its face and name ("Ask Zu"), so it keeps to one line in the narrow aside in every language. Escape leaves where leaving loses nothing.
- What a screen shows, and what the learner needs to answer it, is part of it and stays in the column at every size, read in the same order as on a phone: a question's situation, picture, chart or data table right above it, a drill's rule over its question, an essay's instruction over the box (which, from `lg`, fills the rest of the screen and scrolls inside, so the theme stays in view while writing), a reading screen's picture leading it and its chart or activity after its words. From `lg` a task can have an aside (`getTaskAsideRootProps`): a quiet rail at the window's right edge that runs its full height behind one hairline, as the app's aside does and as wide (300px, 320px from `xl`; a second column's measure, or half the window, for a long shared text), so the task has the rest of the window: its header's row, its column, centered in that room, and its action bar, whose hairline runs up to the rail. The rail's header row is level with the task's (its label on the left, a quiet mono fact on the right) and its foot is level with the action bar (the buddy's composer, Hand in). It draws no panel: its label and words sit on the page on one left edge with the buddy's composer, at the app's 14px, a step quieter than the column, so a rail with only the buddy reads as the page's edge, never an empty box, and a body taller than the screen scrolls on its own and fades out at its foot. It holds what helps, never what's needed to answer: "From the lesson" (the last reading screen) beside a lesson's question, a passage, case, chart or recording several questions share, an essay's motivating texts (with the last grade while rewriting), the session's queue, a mock's answer sheet, what the Trickster will ask, and the buddy at its foot. Under `lg` the same content sits in the column (a lesson's "From the lesson" folded into one row under the question and its result that names the screen it holds, opening in place; none under a question asked before its explanation, which offers "Explain first") or a drawer; `TaskAsideContent` renders it once. Once the task is done the aside stays, so the column never moves, and holds what sits beside the end (a lesson's summary, a quick explanation's "In 3 sentences", a session block's queue, which then names what Continue opens in place of the phone's "Up next" row, kept on a wide screen only when an offer under it is about that block, as "Already know this? Take the test" is), never left empty while there's something to keep.
- Dense results become `Steps`: one card per thing to say, progress segments, Continue (Enter) and Back; a step with nothing to say is skipped. A result's first step is the one number or verdict that matters, its facts as `StatTiles`. A step that sits in the aside from `lg` (`beside`) renders both arrangements on the server, so a reload at any width paints the right one.
- Intros: an anchor (such as the Trickster's art), a big title, two or three fact chips, at most one callout with the rule, then the state and its action.
- Onboarding (`/start`, a new goal's questions, a course's start) is not a task: it's a page of the frame for everyone, visitors included (`OnboardingPage`), with no tab bar under `lg` and no tab marked in the sidebar. But it asks one thing at a time, as a task does, so it's laid out like one: each screen's question is the page's title, at the top, and what it asks, its answer and its next step read down one column, a long question included (its passage, table or chart, then the stem, its options and Confirm). The column fills a phone's or a tablet's like any page and from `lg` keeps a task's measure (`max-w-column`) centered in the page between the sidebar and the aside, as the player's questions sit, so a wide screen leaves even room around the question instead of a strip on one edge. Its row is the page's bar, across the page as every page's is (`OnboardingPageBar`), so it never moves between screens and its ends sit in the page's corners as a task's ✕ and ⋯ sit in the window's: on the left, where every page's way back sits, the way back to the previous screen, a chevron under `lg` and "‹ Back" from `lg` in a breadcrumb's face; the step dots in the middle, over the column, the one place the step shows; and on the right, on the page's edge beside the aside (over it, a "⋯" read as the aside's own menu), one "⋯" at every size (`StartOverMenu`): "Finish later" (your answers are kept) and "Start over" (clears your answers), which asks first, so every step has a way out that keeps the answers and says so (an ✕ there read as closing, and "Start over" in words read as starting the test over beside placement's Start). Where there's no way back (the first screen), the bar's left holds an ✕ under `lg`, named "Leave" as a task's close is, out to the app page the learner left for onboarding with the answers kept, as "Finish later" is (Today says the plan needs a few more answers and leads back), so going back and going out never look alike (Escape presses whichever is there); the bar's end holds a visitor's "Log in". From `lg`, where the sidebar holds the way out, a screen with nothing else in its bar has none. A test's question (placement, a language's level test) is answered for good, so its bar keeps only the way out and the progress (`OnboardingTestQuestion`): the ✕ under `lg`, no way back and no "⋯", whose Start over reads over a question as starting the test over, and in the dots' place how far into the test the learner is, never a total it might not keep: placement's share of the day's few minutes (core's `getPlacementBudgetSpent`: its answers and answering time against the day's limits, ending sooner once it's sure) and a level test's answers over the most it asks. Placement's start says it has no score and that "I don't know yet" is a fine answer, and its way around says what it does ("Skip and start from the basics"). A placement question's eyebrow says what it's about and its number ("ENEM · Question 3"), and "I don't know yet" sits beside Confirm, as on a session's placement question, with "Stop here and see my plan" under them. What needs room in an answer (the goal's field on one row) asks the width it has (`@container`), since the same field sits in Today's section too. From 72rem the rail ("How it works", then "Your plan so far" filling in with each answer) sits in the page's aside; under it each screen shows what it needs from the rail itself (the plan so far as chips over the time a day). The plan's reveal is a detail page, where the plan begins: Start and Adjust beside its title on the page's left edge, what fits and the choices in the aside. Today without a goal shows the same flow under its own title, on Today's left edge (`OnboardingEmbedded`). A single-choice question moves on shortly after a pick and announces it; facts the learner checks ("Here's what I understood") stay labelled rows on phones, never bare chips.

## Structure and progress

- Every goal shows its structure in the learner's terms: an exam's subjects and topics in the notice's own words and order (grouped as the notice groups them), a course's modules, a language's levels and units, each with honest progress.
- **One measure per goal, the whole and its parts alike.** A percentage beside a goal or one of its subjects always means the goal's measure, so the overall number reads as its subjects together and finishing every lesson reads as finished:
  - An exam reads **Preparation** (coverage times how well it's known, see core's `preparation-math.ts`), and each subject its own preparation by the same rule (`SyllabusSubject.preparation`). The goal's number is its subjects' together, each weighted by its share of the exam (by importance when the exam has no shares; core's `measurePreparation`), so it always sits within them, however thin the evidence. Each subject stays under 75% until its harder topics are answered and a test is taken, so the whole can pass 75% while one subject is held: copy states the hold per subject, never as a cap on the whole, and Main shows numbers, never the stage. A subject with too few new questions answered counts no mastery yet: its page says Mastery "Shows after a few practice questions" until then and "6 of 8 right" after, the way the sheet's Mastery does for the whole. The bar is one fill; the subjects' header says "Preparation". Lessons show only as counts ("3 of 662 lessons", "25 lessons skipped"), never as a percentage.
  - Every other goal reads **Progress**: its plan's lessons studied or skipped as known (`SyllabusView.lessons`, the modules' bars added up, "6 of 17 lessons" under the number), each module the same (`CoverageBar`: studied, known, new, with its legend). The lessons are the ones the plan holds (core's `isInLessonScope`): what the learner's time and date fit, never the graph's full depth, so finishing the plan reads 100% and a change to the time or date changes the count, never what was studied. What the time leaves out is the coverage suggestion's to say. Mastery, memory and weekly challenges show how well it's known, in the number's sheet, without moving it.
  - A language goal reads its **level** ("A2+ → B1+", `LevelValue`) on the Plan and Today alike.
  - The Plan's numbers, its subjects, Today's aside, a subject's page and the session summary all use the goal's measure, with the same names, bars and shares.
- A subject's page answers "how far am I, what's next, and is all of it covered?": its name as the notice writes it with "Continue" beside it, its chapters (the next one marked), the notice's topics as the notice's own outline (codes dropped, each ticked and opening to the chapters that teach it, "Appears a lot" only when past exams set some apart, with where that comes from under the list), and in its aside its measure (an exam subject's "Preparation" bar and share, with the Mastery under it; a module's coverage, "Studied 14% · Known 6% · New 80%"), its facts, its mind maps and "Ask {buddy} about this subject". A subject left out of the plan says so, and why; an exam subject the goal still counts (left out for time, its basics passed, its bases skipped: the exam tests it) keeps its preparation, muted at its Plan row's end beside "Not in your plan" and on its page, so the goal's number reads as the subjects it lists. One the learner took out isn't counted and shows none.
- Time reads against that structure as a compact roadmap: bands sized by their days, one date rule (`Jan 24` within twelve months, `Jan 24, 2028` beyond, `~Jan` for an estimate), and each band's details on demand; it never replaces the structure with a long list of chapters. A phase named like one of its chapters goes by its place ("Phase 2") while it's open.
- The pace is the learner's pace against the plan, never whether the plan reaches the goal: "Ahead", "On pace", "Behind" (a count to catch up, in a neutral tone), "Too tight", "Paused". "Finished" means one thing everywhere (core's `isPlanFinished`): every lesson of the plan studied or tested out and every phase's challenge won, so the pace reads "Finished" ("Plan finished" on Today and the pace sheet) only beside 100%, every phase done and "Continue at …", never "13 days ahead". With every lesson done and challenges left, the pace is "Challenges" with "2 challenges left" (that count on Today and the pace sheet too), the path counts only the phases whose challenge was won, and nothing offers what's next yet. A challenge says when it comes as sessions offer them (core's `getBossTimings`, the rule the session duels by), on the path and on its page alike: "In your next session" for each the next session duels (the first one reached, and any whose day came, so none reads a day already gone), "After the challenge before it" for one whose lessons are done behind another still to win (a session duels the next once that one is won), its planned day otherwise. A phase the plan holds nothing of (a date too near for its topics) says "Not in your plan", stays off the roadmap and out of the phases counted done. The map reads each phase's state from the same rule as the Plan (core's `getPhaseStates`), so a finished plan has no current phase anywhere. Plans never "end": one whose date came with lessons left (to do or set aside) is "Past date" with the lessons left ("Past your date" on Today), never finished, and its slot offers the one change that keeps it going, its diff shown before Apply: a goal's own date moves to the day the learner's time covers everything by; an exam's date is the exam's, so every topic goes on after it. Today, with nothing planned past the date, says so instead of a rest day and leads to the Plan, and its week calls those days "after your date", never after the plan; no countdown reads "The day is here" after the day.
- The details behind a number (preparation, progress, pace) open in a sheet: its parts at a glance, the estimate only when there's evidence for it, and at most two things still to do.
- Blocks still to do on Today carry their subject, and the session says which subjects the day covers.

## The buddy

- Every learner has a buddy: until they pick one, it's Zu with round glasses (`resolveProfileBuddy`, `picked: false`), so no screen has a "no buddy" state. Onboarding and Preferences still offer the choice while nothing is picked, and the default buddy's hello offers it too.
- Learners see "buddy" ("Your buddy", "your study buddy"), never "tutor", and the buddy by its name: the tab, and every Ask ("Ask Zu", "Ask Zu about this subject"). Translations use their glossary's word for buddy and their buddy names (the `buddyNames` namespace).
- The learner asks the buddy where the question comes up, each place with its own thread: the buddy tab (the goal's conversation, where the palette's Ask goes too); a lesson screen (the dock in the aside from `lg`, a drawer quoting the question below it, with one-tap "Explain it more simply" and "I want to go deeper" on reading screens and after a miss); a chapter or a unit (Ask in its bar); a subject (a row in the aside's "Also" from 72rem, Ask in its bar below); a session or practice question (the dock in the aside from `lg`, an icon after the main action below it, labelled "Ask {name}" there after a miss); a mock's results (in the same places: the same icon after the main action below `lg`, "Ask {name} about this mock exam" at the aside's foot from `lg`). Opening any of them only reads the thread: nothing is generated until the learner sends, and tapping a suggestion is a send. Before a question is checked the buddy only gives hints; it never helps during a mock still running, a placement question or a timed drill's clock. A lesson screen's conversation shows only where the learner opened it, never on its own when they come back to the screen (its aside leads back to it), and a question coming back at the end of the lesson opens fresh: what was said about its earlier answer returns once it's checked.
- Proposed plan changes appear as a card that says what changes and its effect (and any warning, such as a subject left out or a plan ending early), applied only after the learner's tap. Every proposal, from the buddy, the exam's notice or the plan, waits for its own answer until a newer change about the same thing (the time, the date, the focus, what the notice says) replaces it, and then its card says so instead of offering Apply. Whenever the app has a feature for the wish (a new goal with the learner's words and a matching catalog course, a mock, the chapter test, "Choose where to focus", the Mistakes notebook, a speaking call, statistics…), the buddy offers it as a card with its button instead of describing it, and never points to an outside product for it; a feature the learner's plan doesn't include shows locked, with Plus's notice. The features it can offer are one list in code (`GOAL_TUTOR_APP_TOOLS`), each with its card.

## Mistakes

- The notebook lists mistakes by skill, its tabs "To fix" and "Fixed": on a phone each skill's row opens in place to its mistakes; from `lg` it's an inbox, the skills on the left and the selected one's mistakes beside them. Each entry ends with "Practice this skill" (that skill's open mistakes) and "Open the lesson": the lesson it was made in or, for a question answered outside a lesson, a lesson the learner finished on that skill, else one already written, else the first that teaches it. A fixed entry keeps only "Open the lesson".
- Practice plays each mistake's drill as a session's block does: its note right above the question at every size (the drill's rule, the earlier answer when the question in view is the one it was made on, "Go over the lesson" for a gap; after a gap's idea screen, which says how the drill goes, only the earlier answer), never the right answer; the notebook keeps the mistake as it was made. From `lg` its aside holds only the buddy, at its foot.

## Lesson content

- A lesson shows what it talks about: a picture, a table, a chart or a timeline is rendered from structured content, never described in prose ("na imagem…"). A screen the learner would otherwise have to imagine always shows it, with what the app draws itself first (a table, a chart, a timeline, an activity's diagram or map) and a generated picture only for how something looks that those can't show; screens whose words are clear on their own stay text only, and no picture decorates. Generated media is made once per library step and shared; while it's being made the step says so and waits. Questions outside lessons (practice, placement, tests, challenges, mocks) show their figure the same way, drawn before the question is stored, so they never wait.
- Lessons read like stories. A reading screen leads with its picture (on a phone edge to edge and as tall as the screen allows, in the column's width and capped in height from a tablet up), its chart or activity after its words, in the column at every size, and a new screen slides in from the side the learner heads to; with reduced motion it changes in place. A question has "From the lesson" beside it from `lg`, and below `lg` folded into one row under it and its result ("From the lesson" over the screen's title), opening in place. On a touch screen a swipe left reads on and a swipe right goes back wherever the arrow keys do; a swipe never answers a question, and controls, scrolling and wide tables keep their own gestures.
- After a miss, "From the lesson" marks the sentence the question rests on only when the lesson writer quoted it word for word and code found it on that screen. Lessons without that quote mark nothing; the app never guesses the sentence.
- A lesson gives one personal example at most, on a screen whose idea is abstract or applied, and none when the learner's life adds nothing.
- Audio is always generated in the target language; never the browser's speech engine.
