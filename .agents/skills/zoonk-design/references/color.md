# Color

Zoonk is neutral by default and colorful on purpose. The text color is the primary: the main action is inverted (black in light, white in dark), and focus uses it too. Color goes wherever it carries meaning and makes the design better: what the learner picked and where they are (blue), what a thing is (a kind), how it stands (done, wrong, a flag), progress and stakes (a ring, a filled track, a reward that lights up once earned), and a character's identity. Color is never blanket paint: no brand-colored buttons, no tinted navigation, no decorative gradients, no screen washed in an accent. Ask of every colored element what its color means, and whether it means the same on every screen. A screen still gets its order from size, weight, contrast steps, grouping and spacing first. Tokens live in `packages/ui/src/styles/globals.css` (light, dark and the more-contrast modes).

## Purposeful color, not paint

- **Sections may wear a light wash to read apart.** A page whose parts all sit on white reads as one block, and the eye can't tell where a part starts and ends. A public page can set its major parts on full-width bands, some washed in a light tint (the catalog's course and chapter pages, in the course's subject color, from one map: `SUBJECT_COLORS`), with what's inside (a question, a plan, a list) on its own panel over the wash. A wash is a surface, never a mark: buttons stay the text color, text keeps its tokens and AA, and it turns plain when more contrast is asked for.
- **Color sits where it says something**: a kind's dot, a done check, a progress track, a ring, a reward, a character, the result of an action. It may be bold (a filled track, a solid reward chip, a panel whose state is the point, such as a reward earned) as long as its meaning has a row in the table above and is the same everywhere. A tint belongs on something whose color is its meaning (a checked answer under its ring, a plan diff's lines, a flag's chip, an earned reward), never on a button, a section or a heading for decoration.
- **One meaning per hue, everywhere.** A rose dot is always mistakes, green is always progress made. Before giving something a color, find its row in the table; if none fits, it stays neutral.
- **Blue is the learner's own, and nothing else.** It marks what they picked before it's judged and where they are now, in two forms: a pick is a 2px ring with the tint (`bg-selected-tint`, 8% in light and 16% in dark, so a muted line on it stays AA), and a place is a mark or a ring without the tint. Blue never fills a button, a tab, a link, a heading, a hover or a background; the row the learner is on keeps its neutral fill; one thing per group is picked or current, and a place shows once per view where it can (the Plan's band and next chapter carry it, the path's phase node stays in the text color). A set of toggles where many may be on (a week's study days) takes a 1px blue border on the tint, quieter than a single pick's 2px. Keyboard focus is a separate outline in the text color, so a focused pick shows both. Once checked, a pick turns green or red, and a guess goes back to neutral.
- **A plan change is colored by what it means, not by its sign.** `PlanEffectDiff` colors only its outcome line, the lessons, through one rule (`getLessonLineTone` in `plan-diff/line-tone.ts`): lessons a date leaves out are red, lessons added back green, and without a date fewer lessons are green and more are plain. Every date, end or count that moves is neutral: the old value struck through and muted, the new one in the text color. Good news is never red and bad news never green; a mock's offer to skip what it showed you know is green. The tone follows the same date context as the line's words (`byDate`), so words and color always agree: a proposal says "Leaves out" or "Adds" in the words it has everywhere, so what it would take out is red and what it adds green. A worked answer's diff (your step, the right one) keeps red and green, since there they mean wrong and right.
- **Never hue alone.** Two kinds that can meet in a list differ in shape too (`KindMark`'s icons), so a red-green colorblind learner tells a review from a mock, and review and practice, which share amber, still read apart.
- **A list of one kind shows state, not kind.** Where every row is the same kind (the exam's mocks, the notebook's skills), a kind's dot would say nothing: each row wears its `StatusMark` (done, next, ahead) or nothing.
- **A list scans like Today's queue**: each row's leading mark says what it is or where it stands (a kind's icon, a `StatusMark`, the Trickster's face, a flag), the title is in the text color and the rest is quiet. A lesson's dot is neutral, so the colored dots are the kinds that differ from the day's default. The same thing wears the same mark wherever it appears (a mission has its kind's dot in its card on Today and in the buddy's day).
- **Done reads the same in every list**: the done mark takes the place of the row's dot (Today's queue, the session's queue, a mission, the landing's example day) or fills the ring a list ticks (a routine, a call's goals), and the name is never struck through: the check already says it's done, and a strike would say it twice and make the name harder to read. In a list of work to get through (the day's queue, a routine, the missions), a done name turns muted so what's left stands out.
- **One source per meaning**: a kind's dot, icon and color come only from `KIND_MARKS` in `kind-tile.tsx`, through `KindMark`, `KindDot`, `KindIcon` and `kindTextClass`; Brain Power's violet only from the `brain` and `brain-text` tokens; something done shows `DoneMark` (`status-mark.tsx`), the same green check for a chapter, a lesson, a phase, a block of the day, a finished day, a ticked routine, a call's goal or a mission. No screen spells a kind's color class or draws its own check; a new kind gets its row in `KIND_MARKS` (the type check fails without it) and a hue no other meaning uses (the challenge is lime so violet stays Brain Power's; review and practice share amber, going back over what was learned; conversation and essay share teal, making something yourself, since a cyan read as a second blue).
- **Tint in sRGB**: mix with `color-mix(in srgb, …)`, Tailwind's `/15` opacity or relative color. Mixing in oklch against a transparent black token (`--border` is `oklch(0 0 0 / 7%)`) pulls the hue toward red: a violet border renders pink.
- **Missions**: one "Today's missions" card (`MissionsCard`), the same on Today and in the buddy's day. Each mission wears its kind's icon (`KindMark`, or a character's face) while it's to do and the done mark once done; the day's track, the reward and the mark on the queue row a mission opens are Brain Power's violet. When every mission is done the reward lights up as a solid violet chip, and on Today the card folds to one line. When another goal already earned today's bonus, one quiet line says so and nothing is marked. A day offers only the missions it can complete, and a day that offers none shows no missions at all.
- **Results tint their icons**: a `StatTile`'s icon takes its stat's tone (right answers and the goal's measure green, Brain Power violet through `brain`, Energy amber, time neutral), never its tile.
- **Navigation stays quiet**: every sidebar, tab and menu icon is one neutral color (muted, the current item on a soft fill in full contrast), including shortcuts to a kind's page such as the notebook or the exam. Kind hues live in content: dots in lists, cards and summaries.
- **Never color alone**: every colored mark has a word, a shape or screen-reader text that says the same.

## Hierarchy without color

- **The main action is the strongest thing on a screen**: inverted, one per area. Secondary actions are outline, tertiary ghost; a link inside a sentence stands out by its weight, in the text color.
- **Three text steps**: `foreground` for titles and body; `muted-foreground` for supporting lines, meta and trailing numbers (AA on every surface); `faint` for section labels, eyebrows, list numbers and axis labels, which repeat or frame what's beside them (a step lighter than muted and still AA on every surface it sits on: the page, cards, the sidebar and a row's highlight; a count on the sidebar's current item takes `muted-foreground`, since its fill is darker; the more-contrast mode raises both to full contrast).
- **Weight and size before color**: titles 620 at 30px (`text-title`), row titles 500 at 14px, descriptions 400 at 12px, labels 550 at 11.5px uppercase (`text-label`); a big mono number anchors a block. Three levels of type in a block at most.
- **Group with spacing and hairlines** before adding a container; a panel holds one self-contained thing. In light the page and panels are both white and a panel reads by its hairline, with the sidebar a step dimmer; in dark the page is a step darker than its panels. The row the learner is on gets the soft neutral fill and its mark is blue; a row's fill is never a color, except a pick's tint.

## Native apps

Apple and Android mirror the same rules with native controls: the tint of prominent buttons and toggles is the label color (black in light, white in dark), not a brand hue, so set Apple's `AccentColor` and Android's Material `primary` to it (and turn Android's dynamic color off, which would bring the wallpaper's hue in). A pick and the learner's place take the same blue as the web, never a button. Kinds keep their hues as named colors on dots and icons; green, red and amber keep their meanings, with text in their darker variants (light `#14764A`, `#C02B2F`, `#9D580C`).
