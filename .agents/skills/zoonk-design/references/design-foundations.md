# Design foundations

Always remember this: "Design is not just what it looks like and feels like. Design is how it works." (Steve Jobs)

## Start with the experience

Jobs's emphasis on the customer's experience and Saarinen's emphasis on context lead to a practical question: what should this product help someone accomplish, and what is getting in their way? Resolve that before arranging controls. A capability earns its place through usefulness in the whole experience. [Jobs](https://allaboutstevejobs.com/videos/misc/wwdc_1997_closing_chat), [Saarinen](https://linear.app/now/output-isn-t-design).

## Simplicity comes from understanding

Ive describes simplicity as bringing order to complexity. Clear concepts, relationships, and behavior make an interface understandable. Removing decoration cannot repair a confusing structure; sometimes a label or additional context makes the experience simpler. [Ive](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2013/06/10Apple-Unveils-iOS-7/), [Apple's design principles](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/250/).

## Spend attention deliberately

A screen can contain useful information without making everything compete. Let the current task lead, with supporting controls quieter but recognizable. Spacing, typography, alignment, and contrast should reveal relationships. Restraint means giving each element appropriate emphasis. [Linear's interface design](https://linear.app/now/behind-the-latest-design-refresh).

## Reduce uncertainty and effort

The difficulty of choosing matters more than a fixed number of options or clicks. Give people clear destinations, helpful defaults, and enough information to decide confidently. Sequence separable decisions; keep comparisons together. Hide optional complexity only when its entry point remains easy to find. Repeated tasks should become efficient as people gain familiarity. [Krug](https://sensible.com/downloads/DMMT-Revisited-sample-chapter.pdf), [Nielsen](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/).

## Make care tangible

Ive's work connects usefulness with approachability; Rams connects aesthetics with everyday well-being. Beauty, warmth, and precision belong in the experience. In software, that care can appear in forgiving interactions, stable layouts, thoughtful language, and satisfying feedback. Choose details that support the intended feeling and remain comfortable over time. [Ive](https://designmuseum.org/designers/jonathan-ive), [Rams](https://www.vitsoe.com/us/about/good-design).

## Learn through making and use

Prototyping helps uncover relationships and constraints that an abstract plan misses. Stay willing to revise the structure when using it reveals friction. Watch what people can accomplish as well as how they react: a polished presentation does not establish an easy experience. [Ive on prototyping](https://www.standard.co.uk/lifestyle/london-life/sir-jonathan-ive-the-iman-cometh-7562170.html), [Krug on observing use](https://sensible.com/you-say-potato-i-say-focus-group/).
