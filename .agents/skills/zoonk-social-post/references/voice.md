# Voice

In the owner's words, posts should sound like talking to people, "just showing something cool, showing what problems this solves, and how it's useful". Not engagement bait, not silly. Short, because "it's social media and people don't read long posts". Positive, with no criticism of other tools. The first drafts were "too boring", "not very personal" and "too much like marketing instead of sounding natural"; the owner wants them "closer to the reader, more natural, and a little cooler".

## The rules

- **The first line says what it is.** Someone who reads only that line knows what Zoonk does or what's new. No warm-up, no question to the reader, no "big news".
- **One concrete example.** A real sentence a learner would type, or one moment of the app, beats a list of features. "I want to speak Spanish before I move to Madrid in June." makes the product obvious in a way "personalized learning plans" never does.
- **Say it the way you'd say it out loud.** If a friend would raise an eyebrow at a sentence, rewrite it. Everyday words, the app's real labels, no adjectives doing a job the example should do.
- **Short.** One idea per post. A follow-up gets its own post or reply.
- **Positive.** Say what Zoonk does; never what others get wrong.
- **Goals most people recognize:** a language before a move, the SAT, the ENEM, a career change. The app's own suggestions per language are the safe choice.
- **The link goes in the post** as `zoonk.com`, on its own line at the end.

## Two voices

**Zoonk's accounts** speak as the product: "we" for the team, "you" for the reader. A launch can open with "Introducing", which company launches use; everything after it is plain.

**The owner's personal account** is first person and only says what the owner has really said or done. The owner's own lines are the best material, such as opening 42 YouTube videos and finishing none, or always wanting an app that tells you what to do today. Don't add a feeling, a number or a story to make a draft land; ask the owner.

## Examples

Good, from the 2.0 launch drafts:

> I always wanted an app where I type what I'm trying to learn and it tells me what to do today.
>
> So we built it.
>
> "I want to speak Spanish before I move to Madrid in June" → a plan for every day until June. Skip a day and it adjusts.
>
> zoonk.com

Personal, first line says the problem, the example does the work, no adjective sells anything.

> Apresentamos o Zoonk 2.
>
> Escreva seu objetivo como você contaria a um amigo: "Quero passar no Enem em novembro e entrar em Medicina na USP."

Zoonk's account in Portuguese: the opening names the release, the example is the one the app suggests in Portuguese.

> We rebuilt Zoonk. You type what you're trying to learn, it tells you what to do today.
>
> zoonk.com

The two-line version, for when the clip does the talking.

Not like this:

- "Zoonk 2.0 is an AI-powered learning platform that delivers personalized study plans tailored to your goals." Marketing words with nothing to picture. Show the sentence and the plan instead.
- "Most study apps leave you on your own. Zoonk doesn't." Criticism of others, and an AI-tell reveal.
- "Want a plan for your goal? Comment PLAN 👇" Engagement bait.
- "Pass the IELTS in March so you can study in London." A goal most readers don't know; the SAT or a language reads instantly.
- "After 10 years of struggling to learn, I finally cracked it." A personal story the owner never told.
- A ten-line post with five features. Pick one; the rest are follow-ups.
