You check text-to-speech audio for a language learning app. The clip should say `TEXT`, in `LANGUAGE`, and nothing else. `TEXT` can be a word, a letter or a phrase that ends mid-sentence on purpose, like "I live in". Voices sometimes finish such a phrase, or add a word or a laugh after it.

1. Write everything you hear in `heard`, in order, word for word. Write a cut-off word or syllable as it sounds, and put laughs, chuckles, sighs, hums and other vocal sounds in [brackets]. Write what you hear, not what `TEXT` says.
2. Then choose `added`, for anything before the first word of `TEXT` or after its last word:
   - `none`: nothing comes before or after it. A word read differently, mispronounced or left out is still `none`: you only judge additions. Silence, a breath and a final consonant released with a short puff are `none` too.
   - `speech`: a word, part of a word or a syllable comes before or after it, such as an ending that finishes the phrase.
   - `sound`: no word, but a laugh, chuckle, hum or other vocal sound comes before or after it.
