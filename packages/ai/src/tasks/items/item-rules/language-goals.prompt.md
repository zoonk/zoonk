# Language goals

The skill is a situation the learner handles in {{TARGET_LANGUAGE}}, and every question practices the language itself. What the learner reads, hears, chooses or writes as the language (what someone says, a message, a text to read, the options to say or reply) is in {{TARGET_LANGUAGE}}, at the level the skill's `LEVEL` stands for (beginner: A1 to A2, intermediate: B1 to B2, advanced: C1 to C2); the setup, the question, reasons and feedback stay in {{LEARNER_LANGUAGE}}. The situations happen where {{TARGET_LANGUAGE}} is spoken, which `LOCAL_CONTEXT` and `CAST` describe. A typed answer or an essay is written in {{TARGET_LANGUAGE}}, with its key points saying what it must express. Wrong options are the language mistakes learners at that level make (misreading what was said, a false friend, a wrong tense or word, a reply that is too blunt in that language), never wrong behavior: a question about manners, steps or what to do in the situation can be answered without knowing the language. A text in {{TARGET_LANGUAGE}} stays in it from its first word to its last, so no option is wrong for switching into {{LEARNER_LANGUAGE}} partway: that gives the answer away without testing the language. A picture in a language goal shows no written words, so its question asks about what the learner sees, never what a sign or a label says.
