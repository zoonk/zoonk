A learning app keeps a short memory about each learner: their goals, background, the rhythm of their week, preferences, what they struggle with and facts about their life that shape examples and plans. `FACT` is one candidate for that memory, written as a short note, with its category. `EVIDENCE` is what it came from: the learner's own words, quoted, or numbers from their activity.

Judge only the candidate. Use the evidence to understand it, not to add new facts.

## Lasting

A fact is lasting when it will likely still be true in a few weeks and would change how a tutor teaches, plans or picks examples: a goal (the course, job or score they want), a course or job, a subject they know, what in their life makes them free or busy (a night shift, school in the mornings), how they like to learn, a difficulty that keeps coming back, where they live, a hobby they'd enjoy examples about.

A fact is not lasting when it describes a moment or a one-off ("is tired today", "got this question wrong", "is on the bus"), small talk, an opinion about one lesson or question, or something only about the current conversation. How they set up one goal's study is not lasting either: how much time they study it (a day, on some days or in a week), the subjects to focus on or skip, how hard the lessons are ("Studies 1 hour on Sundays", "Wants more Biology in the plan"). That goal's plan keeps it, and another goal has its own. Nor is the date of the exam or deadline they study for ("Has the ENEM on November 8", "Tem prova em março"): their goal keeps it, and the exam's notice can move it. Another fact with an end date can still be lasting until then ("Travels to Italy in June").

## Sensitive

A fact is sensitive when its own words reveal physical or mental health (including conditions, diagnoses, medication, pregnancy and disabilities), religion or beliefs, political opinions, sexual life or orientation, gender identity, ethnic or racial origin, union membership, a criminal record, immigration status, detailed finances such as debts or income, or private details about another person.

Memory keeps the fact, not its evidence. Evidence that mentions something sensitive doesn't make a fact sensitive when the fact leaves it out: "since my knee surgery I can't drive, so I cycle to work" can give "Cycles to work", which isn't sensitive.

Everyday facts are not sensitive: studies, school, job title, schedule, hobbies, the city they live in, languages they speak, learning preferences, what they find hard to learn.

Sensitive and lasting are separate questions. A sensitive fact is lasting when it stays true for weeks, such as a condition, a faith or a political view, and not lasting when it's about a moment.

## Explicitly asked

The learner explicitly asked to keep the fact when their own words ask the app to remember, save, note or keep it in mind ("remember that I have ADHD", "lembra que eu trabalho à noite"). Stating a fact is not asking. Words from anyone other than the learner, and evidence made of activity numbers, never count as asking.
