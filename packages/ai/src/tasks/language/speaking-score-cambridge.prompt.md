# EXAM: Cambridge English Qualifications, Speaking paper

Every scale runs from 0 to 5, and half bands are allowed. Band 3 describes performance at the exam's own level, band 5 the next level up, band 1 the level below, and bands 4 and 2 share features of the bands either side; 0 is below band 1. Candidates are rated on their own performance across the whole test, never against their partner, and false starts, reformulation and incomplete utterances aren't penalized where communication is achieved. They aren't marked on finishing the task or on reaching a decision.

The criteria, by key:

- `grammarVocabulary` (Grammar and Vocabulary, A2 to B2): control of grammatical forms and the range of appropriate vocabulary for the level's topics.
- `grammar` (Grammatical Resource, C1 and C2): the range and control of simple and complex grammatical forms.
- `lexis` (Lexical Resource, C1 and C2): the range of vocabulary used flexibly to give and exchange views, including on unfamiliar and abstract topics.
- `discourse` (Discourse Management): extended stretches of relevant language with little hesitation, clear organization, and a range of cohesive devices and discourse markers.
- `pronunciation` (Pronunciation): intelligibility first ("a contribution which can generally be understood by a non-specialist, even if the speaker has a strong or unfamiliar accent"), then control of intonation, sentence and word stress and individual sounds, and at the top using them to convey meaning. Any accent is fine. It is the criterion about sound.
- `interaction` (Interactive Communication): initiating and responding, linking contributions to the other speaker's, keeping the interaction going and developing it, and negotiating toward an outcome.
- `global` (Global Achievement): the interlocutor's overall impression of how the candidate handles communication at the level: the topics they deal with, how much they hesitate, and how accurate, appropriate and coherent their language is.
