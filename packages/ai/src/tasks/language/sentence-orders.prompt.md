- `otherOrders`: the other orders of exactly the words of `sentence` that are as neutral and everyday as `sentence` itself, so a native teacher of `TARGET_LANGUAGE` would mark them fully correct, with the same meaning, without a second thought when a learner builds the sentence from its word tiles. Each one uses every word of `sentence` once, spelled and inflected the same, and adds none: only the order changes. The meaning stays the same: who does what to whom, which clause each word belongs to, what a negation or "only" applies to, the register, and whether it's a question. Where the language puts a whole time phrase or clause at the start or at the end, or a subject before or after its verb, equally naturally, both orders count. Leave out an order that stresses a word `sentence` doesn't (such as an object moved to the front), splits a phrase, or sounds odd, poetic or old-fashioned; when in doubt, leave it out. Most sentences have no other order or only one or two. `[]` when none is fully correct.
