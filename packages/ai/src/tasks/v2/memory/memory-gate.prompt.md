A learning app keeps a short memory about each learner: their goals, background, study routine, preferences, what they struggle with and facts about their life that shape examples and plans. `FACT` is one candidate for that memory, written as a short note, with its category. `EVIDENCE` is what it came from: the learner's own words, quoted, or numbers from their activity.

Judge only the candidate. Use the evidence to understand it, not to add new facts.

## Lasting

A fact is lasting when it will likely still be true in a few weeks and would change how a tutor teaches, plans or picks examples: a goal or deadline, a course or job, a subject they know, when they can study, how they like to learn, a difficulty that keeps coming back, where they live, a hobby they'd enjoy examples about.

A fact is not lasting when it describes a moment or a one-off ("is tired today", "got this question wrong", "is on the bus"), small talk, an opinion about one lesson or question, or something only about the current conversation. A fact with an end date can still be lasting until then ("Has the ENEM on November 8").

## Sensitive

A fact is sensitive when it reveals physical or mental health (including conditions, diagnoses, medication, pregnancy and disabilities), religion or beliefs, political opinions, sexual life or orientation, gender identity, ethnic or racial origin, union membership, a criminal record, immigration status, detailed finances such as debts or income, or private details about another person.

Everyday facts are not sensitive: studies, school, job title, schedule, hobbies, the city they live in, languages they speak, learning preferences, what they find hard to learn.

## Explicitly asked

The learner explicitly asked to keep the fact when their own words ask the app to remember, save, note or keep it in mind ("remember that I have ADHD", "lembra que eu trabalho à noite"). Stating a fact is not asking. Words from anyone other than the learner, and evidence made of activity numbers, never count as asking.
