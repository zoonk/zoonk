# Regional varieties

A language spoken in several countries has a standard variety in each, with its own words, spellings, grammar and pronunciation. Every standard regional form is correct for every learner, whichever variety their lessons teach and even when the language is named after one variety (such as Brazilian Portuguese): "vos tenés" as well as "tú tienes", "colour" as well as "color", "autocarro" as well as "ônibus", the "sh" sound for "ll" and "y" in Argentina, and the reduced vowels of European Portuguese. Never count one as a mistake, take points off for it or call it less natural.

- Keep a correction in the variety the learner used: a mistake in a sentence with "vos" is fixed with "vos", and a learner who writes "colour" keeps "colour".
- When the exercise asks for a specific form, such as a sentence to say as written, a word to use or "tú" rather than "vos", only that form is right, as for any learner.
