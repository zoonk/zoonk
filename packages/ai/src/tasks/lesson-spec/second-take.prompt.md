# Second take

Learners studied the lesson in `FIRST_LESSON_MAP`, which teaches the skill in `OUTLINE_SKILLS`, some of them twice, and still miss it in the ways `WRONG_ANSWERS` lists. This lesson teaches the same skill another way: someone who read an explanation twice and still misses the idea needs a new way in, and the same explanation in other words won't help. It's for learning, light and quick: plan exactly one lesson of about 4 minutes and {{SECOND_TAKE_SCREENS}} screens, each screen one idea or one decision, and never split it, whatever "Size rules" say. `COURSE_TITLE` and `CHAPTER_TITLE` say where the first lesson sits, so cases and words fit that subject.

- `opensOn`: the number of the wrong answer in `WRONG_ANSWERS` the lesson opens on, the most tempting one whose reasoning the skill's core idea answers.
- Its only skill is the skill in `OUTLINE_SKILLS`, with its name kept, at the same `LEVEL`.
- **The opening.** The scene and the guess are both about that one wrong answer. The scene shows someone giving it in a new case of your own and getting a result it can't explain, and ends on the question the lesson answers. The guess comes right after it, as the second screen, instead of after the first explanation: another concrete case where that same wrong answer is the tempting option. Learners have met the idea already, so they have enough for a hunch.
- **Back to the scene.** Once the idea is taught, the lesson comes back to the scene's own case and settles it: what the person should have done and why their result happened. The scene's question is what keeps the learner reading, so leaving it open breaks that promise. Settle it on a screen the lesson already has, such as the worked example working the scene's case, so it costs no extra screen.
- **Why it fails, another way.** Read how `FIRST_LESSON_MAP` explained the idea and take another route to it: another representation (a table, a number line, a timeline, a template to explore or a picture where it used words or a formula; a case or a story where it stated a rule; a comparison from another part of life where it used one), another starting point, or the idea seen from the side of the mistake. The explanation right after the guess shows the wrong answer failing in one concrete case, with small numbers or one short real example, before any rule is named; the rule comes after it, on its own screen. The fact that the wrong answer's `Why it's wrong` rests on is said on a screen, so the learner sees why it fails, not only what's right instead.
- **The two ideas apart.** A wrong answer is often another idea learners confuse with this one (a neighboring rule, the everyday meaning of a word, a related quantity). One check makes the learner decide, in a small case, which of the two applies, with the other as its tempting option.
- **A step the learner does.** One worked example of the skill's reasoning on a new case, followed by a check where the learner does a similar one with less help.
- **Only what the confusion needs.** Teach the core idea, the conditions the wrong answers miss and what tells the confused ideas apart. Leave out the first lesson's other points, its extensions and its rare cases: a learner who keeps missing the core gains nothing from them yet. Decide for each of `WRONG_ANSWERS` whether this lesson answers it: one it answers is taught on a screen and practiced in a check; one it leaves out stays out of every screen, the application included.
- **Every idea checked.** Each idea the lesson teaches gets a check of its own before the application, so a learner who misses it sees which idea failed while its screen is fresh. No explanation or worked example comes after the last check, and the application only combines ideas the checks practiced.
- **New checks.** Every check and the application is new: never a question, case or set of numbers from `FIRST_LESSON_MAP` or `WRONG_ANSWERS`, and a case is new only when its situation changes, not a word or a number in it. Where one of `WRONG_ANSWERS`' mistakes fits a check, it's that check's tempting wrong answer, in the new case. Each check is decided by a rule or fact an earlier screen of this lesson states.
- **Plainer words.** The plainest words `LEVEL` allows, in short sentences, and every term explained again in everyday words before any screen uses it, even one the first lesson explained. Plainer never means less true: when the plain version of a rule would be false in a case learners meet (a condition, an exception, a limit), the condition stays, in plain words.
- **Never a paraphrase.** Never restate an explanation of `FIRST_LESSON_MAP` in other words, never copy one of its sentences into a brief and never reuse one of its cases. This lesson explains that lesson's idea again in full, so the rule of "The chapter's other lessons" against explaining an idea again doesn't apply to it.
- **The learner's own example.** The app can add one sentence from the learner's own life under the explanation that states the idea itself. End that explanation's brief by naming where that example goes: an everyday setting outside the subject where the same idea happens.
- **Short enough to finish.** The learner is tired, on a phone, and has already spent two lessons on this skill; they finish this one only if it feels short. Plan the fewest screens the confusion needs, never a screen to reach a count, and keep each screen light: a worked example shows only the moves the confusion turns on, with a move that repeats shown once and then counted rather than written out each time, and every question, the application included, has short options a learner tells apart at a glance, each one answer, never two answers paired in one option.
- The usual rhythm holds otherwise: a check after at most 4 explanation or worked example screens, and the application last. At most one activity, and only one that fits the skill as "Activities" says; visuals only where they teach, as "Visuals" says.
- `title`: the idea in a few plain words, seen from this lesson's angle, in sentence case, never `LESSON_TITLE` word for word. Learners see it next to the first lesson, so it says what's different about this one.
- `description`: one plain sentence on the new way this lesson shows the idea.

In the final check, the guess is the second screen, right after the scene, the scene's case is settled later, every taught idea has its own check before the application, each wrong answer is either taught and checked or left out, every option is one short answer, and the lesson has {{SECOND_TAKE_SCREENS}} screens, as few as the confusion needs.
