Decide whether the picture planned for one screen of a short lesson teaches, or only decorates.

`LESSON` names the lesson. `SCREEN` is the planned screen: its kind (a hook, an explanation, a worked example, a check, an activity or the application) and its brief. `PICTURE` is the picture planned for it. `PREVIOUS_PICTURE` is the picture planned for the screen before, or "none".

The brief is the planner's note to the writer, not the words the learner reads, so it often describes what the picture shows. That alone never makes the picture decorative. Ask instead: if the screen showed only words, would the learner have to imagine how something looks, is built or is arranged to understand it or to answer?

A picture teaches when:

- The screen is about how something looks, is built, is arranged or moves: an organ or a cell and its parts, a machine, a molecule, a map with places, an artwork or a building, an unfamiliar part of an animal's body, a pulley and where the rope pulls.
- It draws an idea the way the field draws it, which the learner would otherwise picture wrong or not at all, such as field lines around a magnet or a light ray bending in water.
- It compares two things whose difference is seen, such as two artworks, two buildings, two charts drawn differently, or a before and after of a shape or a scene.
- The learner answers by looking at it: which of two drawings labeled 1 and 2 is right, which numbered part, which caption fits a scene, where something sits. The screen then asks about the picture instead of describing it.
- It draws a setup the learner works from, such as blocks on a ramp or furniture in a room, with its labels: those numbers mark where things are, not amounts to count.

A picture only decorates when:

- The words let anyone picture it correctly, such as an everyday situation (a glass left in the sun, a dog in a park, two coins on a table, a person holding a phone) or a few plain shapes the words name (a square beside a circle, three stacked boxes), and the screen's question doesn't depend on any detail of how it looks.
- It repeats amounts the words give as price tags, bars, coins, icons or groups of people or things (a $50 tag marked -20%, a bar for 10 next to a bar for 15): numbers are a table or a chart the app draws, never a picture.
- It's text drawn as an image: a formula, a derivation, code, a terminal, an app's or a website's screen.
- It shows something every learner already knows the look of (a dog, a coin, a phone, a giraffe's long neck) when the screen isn't about a detail of it they don't know.
- It shows the same thing as `PREVIOUS_PICTURE` with nothing new to see, such as the same view with a label added to the part it already showed.
- It shows the result the learner is asked to work out or predict, such as a value drawn to scale or an outcome colored in, even in a drawing that would otherwise teach.
