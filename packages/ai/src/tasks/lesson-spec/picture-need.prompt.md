Decide whether the picture planned for one screen of a short lesson teaches, or only decorates.

`LESSON` names the lesson. `SCREEN` is the planned screen: its kind (a hook, an explanation, a worked example, a check, an activity or the application) and its brief, what the screen says or asks. `PICTURE` is the picture planned for it. `PREVIOUS_PICTURE` is the picture planned for the screen before, or "none".

A picture teaches when a learner who only read the screen's words would otherwise have to imagine something to understand it or to answer:

- How something looks, is built, is arranged or moves: an organ or a cell and its parts, a machine, a molecule, a map with places, a layout of people or objects, an artwork or a building, an animal's body, a lever and where the forces act.
- A comparison of two things that can only be seen, such as two paintings, two charts drawn differently, or a before and after of a shape or a scene.
- A question about the picture itself: which part is marked, which caption fits, what a sign or a scene shows, when the skill is reading pictures.

A picture only decorates when:

- The screen's words already say everything it shows: a situation described in a sentence (a glass left in the sun, a dog in a park, two coins on a table, a person holding a phone), a hook whose question doesn't depend on seeing anything.
- It repeats numbers the words give, as price tags, bars or icons (a $50 tag marked -20%, a bar for 10 next to a bar for 15, rows of coins): numbers are a table or a chart the app draws, never a picture.
- It's text drawn as an image: a formula, a derivation, code, a terminal, an app's or a website's screen.
- It shows something every learner already knows the look of (a dog, a coin, a phone) when the screen isn't about a detail of it they don't know.
- It shows the same thing as `PREVIOUS_PICTURE`, with nothing new to see.
- It gives away the answer to the screen's own question.
