Decide whether the picture planned for one screen of a short lesson teaches, or only decorates.

`LESSON` names the lesson. `LEARNERS` is `new` when its learners have never seen the subject, and `experienced` when they already know its basics or prepare for an exam. `SCREEN` is the planned screen: its kind (a hook, an explanation, a guess, a worked example, a check, an activity or the application) and its brief, what the screen says or asks. `PICTURE` is the picture planned for it. `PREVIOUS_PICTURE` is the picture planned for the screen before, or "none".

The brief is the planner's note to the writer, not the words the learner reads, so it often describes what the picture shows. That alone never makes the picture decorative.

The app also draws tables, charts, timelines and interactive templates (a process in order, causes and effects, a decision tree, a value on a slider, a number line, a balance, a map, a labeled diagram, a timeline and more). A picture earns its cost only for what has to be seen, or for an idea too complex for any of those to show. It teaches when a learner who only read the screen's words would otherwise have to imagine something to understand it or to answer:

- How something looks, is built, is arranged or moves: an organ or a cell and its parts, a machine, a molecule, a map with places, a layout of people or objects, an artwork or a building, an animal's body, a pulley and where the rope pulls.
- A comparison of two things that can only be seen, such as two artworks, two charts drawn differently, or a before and after of a shape or a scene.
- The learner answers by looking at it: which of two drawings labeled 1 and 2 is right, which numbered part, which caption fits a scene, where something sits.
- It draws a setup the learner works from, such as blocks on a ramp or furniture in a room, with its labels: those numbers mark where things are, not amounts to count.
- A mechanism or a physical setup too complex for any template to show, such as many parts moving together.
- For `new` learners: a thing, a group, a place or a structure the learner doesn't see in daily life, even when the words describe or define it.

A picture only decorates when:

- What it would show is something the app draws better: steps in order, causes and effects, a decision, a value that changes with another, amounts, data or dates. A template, a table, a chart or a timeline teaches that.
- It shows the lesson's story, a person or an everyday situation again, with nothing on it the learner has to see to understand the screen's idea.
- For `new` learners, its main subject is something the screen's words don't name.
- For `experienced` learners, the screen's words already say everything it shows: a situation described in a sentence (a glass left in the sun, a dog in a park, two coins on a table, a person holding a phone), a hook whose question doesn't depend on seeing anything.
- It repeats numbers the words give, as price tags, bars or icons (a $50 tag marked -20%, a bar for 10 next to a bar for 15, rows of coins): numbers are a table or a chart the app draws, never a picture.
- It's text drawn as an image: a formula, a derivation, code, a terminal, an app's or a website's screen.
- For `experienced` learners, it shows something every learner already knows the look of (a dog, a coin, a phone) when the screen isn't about a detail of it they don't know.
- It shows the same thing as `PREVIOUS_PICTURE`, with nothing new to see, even with what the scene was already about now highlighted. The same scene with a different part highlighted (one gear, then the chain it turns) is new.
- It shows the result the learner is asked to work out or predict, such as a value drawn to scale or an outcome colored in, or the answer to the screen's own question, even in a drawing that would otherwise teach.
